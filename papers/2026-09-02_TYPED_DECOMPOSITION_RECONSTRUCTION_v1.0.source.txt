## Abstract

Scientific models often fail not because a new law is immediately required, but because a representation has grouped together states, relations, scales, histories, or properties that are equivalent for one purpose but not for the target currently being predicted, controlled, or explained. This paper formalises **Typed Decomposition-Reconstruction (TDR)** as a prospective research protocol for detecting and testing such hidden scientific conflations. TDR begins from a native representation and a declared target. When materially different target futures, interventions, or closure decisions remain inside one operational address, the method decomposes only the decision-bearing object, types the resulting components and relations, forbids automatic property inheritance across unlicensed relations, reconstructs the model only through explicit carrier or realisation relations, and requires the proposed repair to improve held-out or prospective discrimination beyond strong native alternatives.

The paper does not claim novelty for decomposition, abstraction, typing, causal abstraction, predictive state, sufficient representation, ablation, or model comparison individually. Those ideas have substantial established literatures. The narrower candidate contribution is an ordered cross-domain discipline joining: recursive addressed decomposition; property-specific no-untyped-inheritance; carrier-qualified reconstruction; target-relative future-sufficiency tests; existing-owner subtraction; preserve-or-reopen reuse; and explicit parent-generator accountability. A method-level benchmark is defined by

$$
\boxed{
G_{\mathrm{gen}}(q)
=
F_q(\mathcal G_{\mathrm{TDR}})
-
F_{\mathrm{base}}^{*}(q)
}
$$

where the baseline is the strongest preregistered serious comparator under matched information, time, compute, human assistance, search budget, revision opportunity, and stopping rules. The sign may be negative. A negative value is a loss for TDR on that task, not a hidden success.

A secondary construct, **Wake**, is introduced as a target-relative retained traversal/history variable: the smallest load-bearing residue of prior traversal that changes present interpretation, prediction, or the admissible next transition. Wake is not proposed as a substance or universal memory law; it earns inclusion only when it improves prospective closure beyond simpler current-state descriptions.

The resulting proposal is deliberately falsifiable. TDR should contract to ordinary careful analysis if its decompositions are unstable, hindsight-selected, reproducible only by its originators, no better than native methods, or unable to produce useful distinctions prospectively. Its strongest possible result would not be a new law of nature, but evidence that a specific disciplined search procedure finds decision-bearing hidden distinctions more reliably or efficiently than strong alternatives on fresh scientific tasks.

**Keywords:** scientific representation; typed decomposition; model criticism; causal abstraction; predictive sufficiency; hidden state; cross-scale modelling; prospective testing; falsifiability; research methodology; Wake; MKUFT

## 1. Why a methods paper is needed

A research programme can accumulate local results without yet owning a general method. Conversely, a general method can become visible only after enough local results have exposed the same structural operation from different directions.

The present paper was motivated by a repeated pattern across the later MKUFT methodological modules. The pattern was initially distributed rather than presented as one standalone object. Examples included:

- informational adjacency separated from physical proximity;
- local performance separated from whole-system closure;
- present address equality separated from target-future equivalence;
- functional recovery separated from reversal and exact state restoration;
- residual improvement separated from independent coordinate ownership;
- learning from a failed branch separated from evidence that the parent generator succeeded.

The common move is not that all these domains share one mechanism. They do not. The common move is methodological:

> **An apparent unity is decomposed into decision-bearing typed objects; properties are prevented from crossing between those objects without a licensed carrier; the model is reconstructed only through relations that survive explicit tests; and the resulting representation must change a fresh prediction, intervention, recovery route, discriminator, or closure decision to earn retention.**

This operation was already explicit in MKUFT's recursive address-closure machinery. The purpose of this paper is therefore not to rename an existing capability and present it as newly discovered. It is to extract the method into an independently readable object, subtract established neighbouring work, formalise the prospective burden, and make the method itself lose-able.

The paper makes three levels of claim, which should remain separate.

1. **Descriptive claim:** a recurring typed decomposition-reconstruction discipline exists across the source programme.
2. **Methodological claim:** this discipline can be stated as a reproducible prospective protocol.
3. **Performance claim:** TDR generates useful distinctions better than strong native alternatives.

The first is supported by the public architecture. The second is the object formalised here. The third is **not yet established** and is the target of the benchmark proposed in Section 8.

## 2. What TDR does not claim

A new name is not a novelty argument. The generic ingredients of TDR have important precedents.

Abstract interpretation formalises sound movement between concrete and abstract semantic domains and fixpoint reasoning [8]. Causal-abstraction research formalises low-level/high-level mappings and intervention-preservation requirements [9]. Predictive-state representations define system state in terms of action-conditional future predictions [10]. The information-bottleneck programme studies compressed representations retaining target-relevant information [11]. Causal representation learning further addresses representation choices that expose stable causal structure rather than merely predictive correlations [12]. Statistics, system identification, control, machine learning, and philosophy of science contain many additional forms of model criticism, sufficient representation, latent-state discovery, ablation, and complexity control.

Accordingly, this paper **does not** claim to have invented any of the following by themselves:

- decomposition;
- abstraction;
- typing;
- latent-state or hidden-state search;
- sufficient statistics;
- predictive state;
- causal abstraction;
- intervention-preserving representation;
- coarse-graining;
- ablation or restoration;
- model selection;
- held-out validation;
- history-dependent state augmentation;
- the observation that ambiguous language can conceal category errors.

The narrower candidate contribution is the ordered conjunction:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{residual or ambiguity}
&\rightarrow \text{typed decomposition}
\rightarrow \text{no free inheritance}\\
&\rightarrow \text{carrier-qualified reconstruction}
\rightarrow \text{native-owner subtraction}\\
&\rightarrow \text{prospective target test}
\rightarrow \text{portfolio accountability}.
\end{aligned}
$$

Historical priority for this exact conjunction is not asserted here. The scientific burden is operational: does the conjunction improve discovery or discrimination on fresh tasks beyond strong alternatives under fair resource matching?

## 3. Operational definition of a hidden scientific conflation

The word *conflation* is used narrowly here. It does not mean that two things are metaphysically different merely because a researcher can name them separately. A split matters only relative to a declared target.

Let $\mathcal X$ be a state or instance space and let the native representation be

$$
A_{\Theta}:\mathcal X\rightarrow\mathcal Z_{\Theta},
$$

where $\Theta$ is the typed coordinate family retained by the current model. Two states are equivalent under the native address when

$$
x\sim_{\Theta}x'
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
A_{\Theta}(x)=A_{\Theta}(x').
$$

Let $q:\mathcal X\rightarrow\mathcal Q$ be the target quantity, $\Delta$ the prediction horizon, $U$ the admissible intervention or control class, and $E$ the declared environment class. Let

$$
K^{\Delta}(\cdot\mid x,u,e)
$$

be the physical or model transition kernel, and let $d_Q$ be a preregistered discrepancy on target distributions.

Define the target-relative address residual

$$
\mathcal R_q(\Theta;U,E,\Delta)
=
\sup_{\substack{x\sim_{\Theta}x'\\u\in U,\ e\in E}}
 d_Q\!\left(
 q_*K^{\Delta}(\cdot\mid x,u,e),
 q_*K^{\Delta}(\cdot\mid x',u,e)
 \right).
$$

For tolerance $\varepsilon_q$, the representation is prospectively sufficient for the declared target when

$$
\boxed{
\mathcal R_q(\Theta;U,E,\Delta)
\leq
\varepsilon_q.
}
$$

A **candidate hidden conflation** exists when same-address representatives retain stable target-relevant divergence beyond tolerance:

$$
\mathcal R_q(\Theta;U,E,\Delta)>
\varepsilon_q.
$$

This inequality does **not** prove that a unique missing variable exists. The divergence may instead reflect model error, insufficient data, unmatched preparation, environmental mismatch, measurement aliasing, nonstationarity, or correctly modelled stochastic structure. TDR therefore treats the residual as a diagnostic trigger, not as a certificate for a preferred ontology.

This target-relative definition is essential. Two states may be equivalent for one task and importantly different for another. A higher-scale description may be sufficient for one property while requiring lower or multiscale readdressing for another. A distinction earns scientific status through what it changes, not through how intuitively compelling its label sounds.

## 4. The TDR protocol

TDR has seven stages. Each stage can return null. A null stage is not a failure of scientific practice; it is often the correct outcome.

### 4.1 Freeze the target and fair search envelope

Before inspecting the confirmatory outcome, declare as far as practicable:

- the scientific target or decision;
- the data or task family;
- intervention/environment class;
- primary discrepancy or loss;
- acceptable tolerance;
- information access;
- human assistance;
- compute/tool budget;
- branch or candidate-search budget;
- revision opportunity;
- comparator family;
- stopping rule;
- criteria for success, null, and failure.

Without this boundary, any sufficiently flexible discovery process can look successful retrospectively.

### 4.2 Localise the smallest decision-bearing residual

Do not decompose the entire world because a model missed one target. Identify the smallest representation boundary that could change the result.

The residual can arise from state, relation, history, boundary, environment, preparation, scale, interface, measurement, model regime, or another typed coordinate. Ordinary explanations receive full priority. TDR is weakened, not strengthened, when a native-domain variable closes the residual more simply.

### 4.3 Typed decomposition

Let $O$ be the apparent object under analysis. Decomposition may expose

$$
D(O)
=
\{o_i,r_{ij},b_k,v_\ell,p_m\},
$$

where $o_i$ are subobjects, $r_{ij}$ relations, $b_k$ boundaries or scopes, $v_\ell$ versions/states, and $p_m$ properties being asserted or transferred.

Each load-bearing element receives an address such as

$$
T(x)
=
(\text{domain},\text{layer},\text{boundary},\text{version},
\text{role},\text{evidence},\text{status}).
$$

The address tuple is not assumed to be a universal metric. It is an audit object forcing distinctions that can change the inference to remain visible.

### 4.4 No untyped inheritance

Suppose object $A$ is connected to object $B$ by relation $R$:

$$
A\xrightarrow{R}B.
$$

If property $P$ is established for $A$, the inference

$$
P(A)\Rightarrow P(B)
$$

is licensed only when the connecting relation belongs to the admissible carrier class for that property:

$$
\boxed{
P(A)\Rightarrow P(B)
\quad\text{only if}\quad
R\in\mathcal C_P.
}
$$

A relation that carries provenance may not carry evidence. A relation that carries causal influence may not carry identity. Spatial proximity does not automatically carry informational adjacency; informational adjacency does not automatically carry physical write-access. Shared lineage does not automatically carry functional continuity. A successful component does not automatically transfer success to its enclosing whole.

The purpose is not to make every inference impossible. It is to make the bridge explicit enough to attack.

### 4.5 Carrier-qualified reconstruction

Decomposition alone is cheap. The important operation is reconstruction.

Let $\mathcal E$ be the candidate relation set after decomposition. For property $P$, define the licensed edge set

$$
\mathcal E_P^{\star}
=
\{R\in\mathcal E: R\in\mathcal C_P\}.
$$

A reconstructed model $M^{\star}$ may use only the relations whose carrier burden has been met for the property being transferred:

$$
M^{\star}
=
\mathfrak R
\left(
\{o_i\},
\{\mathcal E_P^{\star}\}_{P}
\right).
$$

This is schematic notation, not a universal reconstruction theorem. In a concrete domain, the carrier may be a physical coupling, causal pathway, interface contract, descent relation, measurement channel, lineage condition, or other native mechanism.

The reconstruction step is where TDR differs most from a generic instruction to "look for hidden distinctions." A proposed split that cannot be reconnected through explicit lawful relations may be conceptually interesting but scientifically unusable.

### 4.6 Existing-owner and simpler-representation subtraction

Before declaring a new coordinate, mechanism, or level, test whether the residual is already recoverable through:

1. an existing native variable;
2. a derived combination of existing variables;
3. a coalition of existing measurements;
4. a known causal or mechanistic description;
5. a simpler boundary or preparation variable;
6. an ordinary history variable;
7. a standard abstraction or representation method;
8. another fair native-domain explanation.

For a candidate coordinate $c$, define

$$
\Theta^+=\Theta\cup\{c\}
$$

and the target-relative completion gain

$$
\Gamma_q(c\mid\Theta)
=
\mathcal R_q(\Theta;U,E,\Delta)
-
\mathcal R_q(\Theta^+;U,E,\Delta).
$$

A larger representation is not automatically a better one. Retention requires that the gain survive held-out or prospective testing and not be explained by leakage, target encoding, unfair resource advantage, estimator artefact, or a simpler already-known coordinate.

### 4.7 Preserve, reopen, repair, reduce, or reject

The terminal verdict is deliberately plural:

- **PRESERVE:** the current representation already closes the declared target;
- **REOPEN:** a previously adequate representation fails under a widened target, intervention, or regime;
- **REPAIR:** a new typed distinction improves prospective closure and survives subtraction;
- **REDUCE:** the proposed distinction collapses into known machinery or a simpler representation;
- **REJECT:** the split does not produce stable target-relevant gain.

A method that cannot return *reduce* or *reject* is not a scientific discovery protocol.

## 5. Three worked domain mappings

The following examples demonstrate the operation. They do not imply one shared mechanism across domains.

### 5.1 Physics: informational adjacency versus physical proximity

Consider two systems $X$ and $Y$. One common conflation is to assume that a relation relevant to joint statistics must be identical to spatial proximity. TDR separates:

$$
\mathrm{adjacent}_{I}(X,Y)
\not\Rightarrow
\mathrm{near}_{P}(X,Y),
$$

$$
\mathrm{near}_{P}(X,Y)
\not\Rightarrow
\mathrm{adjacent}_{I}(X,Y).
$$

The split by itself establishes no new physics. A candidate informational relation may affect physical observables only through a declared physical realisation or coupling, schematically

$$
C_{IP}:\mathcal X_I\rightarrow\mathcal X_P,
$$

with source variable, receiving variable, units or normalisation where applicable, measurable consequence, ordinary baseline, and falsifier.

The TDR verdict is therefore:

- useful conceptual distinction: possible;
- direct physical mechanism: not inherited;
- physical claim: earned only through an independently testable coupling;
- if standard quantum formalism already explains all observables without additional predictive gain: the stronger physical claim reduces.

The value of the decomposition is precisely that it can open a relational hypothesis without smuggling causal authority across the boundary.

### 5.2 Biology: same present phenotype, different target futures

Suppose a cell population is classified by a native marker panel $\Theta$. Two cells satisfy

$$
A_{\Theta}(x)=A_{\Theta}(x')
$$

but under matched treatment and environment their target outcomes differ systematically beyond tolerance.

TDR does not infer a mysterious hidden biology. It asks which native distinctions could explain the split: recent stress history, regulatory state, lineage, microenvironment, metabolic condition, epigenetic state, cell-cycle state, measurement aliasing, or model error.

A candidate history marker $h$ is added:

$$
\Theta^+=\Theta\cup\{h\}.
$$

If

$$
\Gamma_q(h\mid\Theta)>\delta_h
$$

on held-out cells, and the effect survives matched preparation, leakage checks, ablation/restoration, and comparison with simpler variables, then $h$ earns target-relative state status.

The claim is not that every cellular state requires history. It is that *history should be retained only when it carries prospective load that the present-state representation cannot recover more simply*.

### 5.3 AI evaluation: residual coordinate versus coalition ownership

Suppose an AI evaluation body $\mathcal Y_n=\{y_1,\ldots,y_n\}$ repeatedly misses a material failure class $r$. Let the best admissible recovery risk from the existing measurement body be

$$
\mathcal L_n(r).
$$

A new candidate coordinate $x$ produces augmented risk

$$
\mathcal L_{n,+x}(r)
$$

and marginal diagnostic gain

$$
\Delta_x(r)
=
\mathcal L_n(r)-\mathcal L_{n,+x}(r).
$$

A positive $\Delta_x$ does not establish an independent coordinate. The existing body must first be tested for direct ownership, derived ownership, and coalition recovery. Candidate-specific annotations may not be smuggled back into the baseline evaluator. If an existing coalition recovers $r$ fairly, the correct result may be an interaction term or improved scoring rule rather than a new dimension.

Again, TDR treats *new label* and *new scientific coordinate* as different objects.

## 6. Wake: target-relative retained traversal history

The repeated-state problem appears in human learning, human-AI collaboration, adaptive control, biology, and other history-bearing systems: two agents or systems may receive the same present input while differing in the retained path information that makes the input actionable.

This paper uses **Wake** as a compact operational term for the smallest retained traversal/history object that still changes the declared present task.

Let $H_{0:t}$ be available traversal history and define a candidate compression

$$
W_t=\Phi(H_{0:t}).
$$

The augmented present state is

$$
S_t^+=(S_t,W_t).
$$

Wake is not assumed to be necessary. It earns target-relative status only if it reduces the relevant residual or changes an admissible transition prospectively. For the predictive case,

$$
\Gamma_q^{W}
=
\mathcal R_q(\Theta;U,E,\Delta)
-
\mathcal R_q(\Theta\cup\{W\};U,E,\Delta).
$$

A candidate Wake is retained only when

$$
\Gamma_q^{W}>\delta_W
$$

under held-out or prospective testing and when no simpler present-state coordinate explains the same gain with lower complexity.

In a decision problem, an equivalent operational test is whether the admissible action set changes:

$$
\mathcal A(S_t\mid I,B,W_t)
\neq
\mathcal A(S_t\mid I,B)
$$

for the same governing intent $I$ and boundaries $B$.

A **Wake Gap** between two readers, agents, or systems is therefore not a universal psychological distance. It is a target-relative difference in retained traversal state that produces materially different interpretation, prediction, or admissible continuation under the same present input.

This terminology is deliberately separated from physical hysteresis. Hysteresis retains its standard domain-specific meaning, for example unequal transition and return thresholds under matched protocol. Wake is an address candidate for retained traversal history. If current state already predicts the target adequately, Wake returns null.

### 6.1 Why Wake matters for scientific communication

Scientific papers compress the author's traversal. A reader sees the final statement without necessarily carrying the failed models, distinctions, or prior constraints that made the statement salient. This can create a Wake Gap: the sentence is locally understandable yet its load-bearing role is not.

The engineering response is not to reproduce the author's entire history. It is to transfer the **minimum sufficient Wake**: enough prerequisites, contrasts, examples, and failure boundaries that the important claim reaches the reader with its intended operational meaning.

This is a communication application of TDR, not evidence for the scientific content being communicated.

## 7. Strongest fair nulls and anti-hindsight controls

TDR is unusually vulnerable to hindsight because almost any complex result can be redescribed as evidence that something had been conflated. The method therefore requires explicit anti-hindsight controls.

### 7.1 Strongest fair comparator

For each task $q$, define a serious comparator family $\mathcal B_q$. Candidate comparators should include, where applicable:

- the native-domain expert workflow;
- generic careful structured analysis without TDR-specific machinery;
- matched AI-assisted research without the focal TDR protocol;
- standard representation/abstraction methods from the native literature;
- matched human-assisted or collaborative conditions where human contribution is load-bearing;
- full-history replay when path-formed structure is claimed;
- another comparator whose success would materially remove the TDR advantage.

The comparator family should be strong but finite. An imaginary method that cannot be implemented or scored fairly is not a stronger scientific null simply because it can be named.

### 7.2 Fresh-evidence rule

A failed prediction may reveal a useful distinction. That is legitimate scientific learning. But the failure cannot simultaneously validate the repair.

The lawful sequence is:

$$
\text{Prediction 1 fails}
\rightarrow
\text{candidate repair generated}
\rightarrow
\text{Prediction 2 frozen}
\rightarrow
\text{fresh evidence}.
$$

The invalid sequence is:

$$
\text{Prediction 1 fails}
\rightarrow
\text{repair explains Prediction 1 retrospectively}
\rightarrow
\text{repair counted as confirmed}.
$$

Failed branches remain in the parent generator's performance record whenever generator merit is claimed.

### 7.3 Common-descent control

Multiple later modules instantiating TDR are not independent confirmations if they descend from one earlier methodological rule. Common descent must remain explicit. Structural recurrence shows reuse and scope; it does not multiply the evidence count.

### 7.4 Resource matching

A method given more time, compute, literature access, expert intervention, model calls, search branches, or revision cycles can appear superior for trivial reasons. The benchmark must match or explicitly price those resources.

## 8. The prospective generator benchmark

The central test is not whether one TDR decomposition can be made persuasive. It is whether the **generator procedure** produces useful prospective distinctions better than the strongest fair alternatives.

Let $\mathcal Q_{\mathrm{hold}}$ be a prospectively frozen task family. For each task $q$, let:

- $\mathcal G_{\mathrm{TDR}}$ be the TDR generator under test;
- $\mathcal B_q$ be the preregistered comparator family;
- $F_q(M)$ be a preregistered higher-is-better portfolio score for method $M$.

The score must include the complete serious attempt portfolio: false positives, failed branches, repairs, search cost, and revisions according to the frozen protocol.

Define

$$
F_{\mathrm{base}}^{*}(q)
=
\sup_{b\in\mathcal B_q}F_q(b).
$$

The **prospective generator gain** is

$$
\boxed{
G_{\mathrm{gen}}(q)
=
F_q(\mathcal G_{\mathrm{TDR}})
-
F_{\mathrm{base}}^{*}(q).
}
$$

The sign has direct meaning:

$$
G_{\mathrm{gen}}(q)>0
$$

supports TDR advantage on that task under the declared envelope;

$$
G_{\mathrm{gen}}(q)\approx 0
$$

supports no demonstrated advantage within uncertainty/tolerance; and

$$
G_{\mathrm{gen}}(q)<0
$$

means the strongest comparator outperformed TDR.

No reinterpretation converts the last case into a confirmatory success.

If task metrics are not commensurable, report the vector

$$
\mathbf G_{\mathrm{gen}}
=
\bigl(G_{\mathrm{gen}}(q_1),\ldots,G_{\mathrm{gen}}(q_m)\bigr)
$$

rather than averaging incompatible quantities.

If all task scores are prospectively normalised to a common direction and scale and weights $w_q$ are fixed before outcome inspection,

$$
w_q\ge0,
\qquad
\sum_q w_q=1,
$$

then

$$
G_{\mathrm{gen}}^{\mathrm{agg}}
=
\sum_q w_q G_{\mathrm{gen}}(q)
$$

may be used as a portfolio statistic.

A finite generator-level hypothesis can then be specified as

$$
H_{\mathrm{gen}}:
\mathbb E\!\left[G_{\mathrm{gen}}^{\mathrm{agg}}\right]
>
\delta_{\mathrm{gen}},
$$

with sampling model, uncertainty interval, replication burden, and decision rule defined by the actual benchmark.

### 8.1 Equation status

The expressions above are **methodological/statistical scaffolds**, not physical laws. Before the task family, baselines, score, resources, stopping rules, and uncertainty model are prospectively frozen, they remain design equations. They become empirical statistical objects only when instantiated and run.

A positive generator score would support a methods claim. It would not validate every MKUFT physical hypothesis, nor would it convert TDR into a law of nature.

## 9. A minimum prospective experiment

A useful first experiment should be deliberately smaller than the ambition of the framework.

### 9.1 Task selection

Select a finite set of fresh problems in domains where:

- the target outcome is independently scoreable;
- a native baseline exists;
- hidden representation insufficiency is plausible but not known in advance;
- confirmatory outcomes can be protected from the generators;
- candidate splits can be operationalised without privileged answer leakage.

Examples might include controlled dynamical systems with known hidden state, biological datasets with prospectively protected outcomes, scientific model-selection tasks, or AI evaluation residuals with held-out failure labels.

### 9.2 Conditions

At minimum compare:

1. native-domain baseline;
2. generic structured-analysis baseline;
3. matched AI-assisted baseline without TDR;
4. TDR condition under the same information/tool budget.

If Wake/path contribution is under study, add a full-history replay condition separately. Do not collapse TDR merit and human-AI coupling merit into one statistic.

### 9.3 Pre-outcome TDR record

For each task, before reveal of the protected outcome, record:

- apparent object/address;
- predicted hidden conflation, if any;
- proposed typed split;
- properties whose inheritance is blocked;
- proposed carrier/reconstruction relation;
- ordinary/native alternatives;
- candidate discriminating observation;
- expected direction of improvement;
- failure condition;
- stopping decision if no split is justified.

A method earns credit for correctly returning **no useful split** when the native representation is already sufficient.

### 9.4 Outcome classes

Each task should finish in one of at least four classes:

- **prospective hit:** predicted distinction materially improves the protected target beyond fair baseline;
- **native-owner reduction:** the distinction is real but is already owned more simply by native machinery;
- **null:** no material prospective gain;
- **failure/false positive:** TDR predicts a consequential distinction that does not survive.

This prevents a portfolio containing only successful descendants from impersonating the full generator record.

## 10. Failure modes that would weaken or kill the broad method claim

TDR should be reduced or rejected as a general generator if one or more of the following persists under fair testing:

1. **Retrospective-only success.** Useful decompositions appear only after the answer is known.
2. **Native sufficiency.** Domain experts or standard methods recover the same distinctions with equal or lower cost.
3. **Instability.** Independent analysts applying the stated protocol generate materially incompatible load-bearing decompositions without a discriminator.
4. **Complexity inflation.** The method repeatedly adds coordinates or layers that improve in-sample fit but not protected outcomes.
5. **Carrier failure.** Proposed decompositions cannot be lawfully reconstructed into a model that changes measurable or decision-relevant consequences.
6. **Common-descent inflation.** Correlated descendant applications are counted as independent evidence.
7. **Resource confounding.** Apparent gains disappear under matched time, compute, information, or expert support.
8. **No-abstention problem.** The method always finds a hidden distinction and cannot prospectively return *none*.
9. **Hydra behaviour.** Every failed branch generates a repair and the parent claims success regardless of portfolio cost.
10. **Wake mystification.** History is added after every miss without prospective gain or simpler-state subtraction.
11. **Domain smuggling.** A structural analogy is silently promoted into a common mechanism across physics, biology, AI, or social systems.
12. **No generator advantage.** $G_{\mathrm{gen}}$ is consistently null or negative against strong comparators.

Any locally valid equation, diagnostic, or scientific result remains valid at its own tested address even if the broad TDR generator claim fails.

## 11. Why cross-domain use is possible without cross-domain mechanism claims

TDR is intended to be domain-general only at the **methodological** level.

The same procedural questions can be asked across domains:

- What exact object is being treated as one?
- Which distinction changes the target?
- What is the domain-native unit or value space?
- What scale and boundary apply?
- Which property is being transferred?
- What relation is claimed to carry it?
- What native mechanism realises that relation?
- What fresh observation would discriminate the repair?
- What result would make the split unnecessary or wrong?

The answers must remain native to the domain.

A biological regulatory relation is not thereby an informational coupling in the physical MKUFT sense. A graph edge in an AI architecture is not a spacetime adjacency. A cross-scale similarity does not establish one causal mechanism. TDR transports **questions and audit burdens**, not mechanism by metaphor.

This constraint is central to the paper. Without it, a cross-domain method becomes a semantic smuggling device.

## 12. Reader and collaborator reproducibility

A methods paper is useful only if another researcher can apply the method without the originators supplying unstated intuitions.

A reproducible TDR report should contain:

1. target and scope;
2. native representation;
3. protected outcome or held-out split;
4. residual/failure that triggered decomposition;
5. proposed typed decomposition;
6. property-carrier table;
7. native-owner and simpler-model subtraction;
8. reconstruction rule;
9. prospective prediction;
10. resource budget and comparator family;
11. outcome;
12. whether the candidate was preserved, reopened, repaired, reduced, or rejected;
13. lineage if the result generated a descendant hypothesis.

The protocol should be teachable to an independent team. If the method works only when an originator "feels" the right split, the broad reproducibility claim fails even if the originator is occasionally insightful.

This is also where Wake becomes experimentally interesting. A complete-history replay condition can ask whether explicit records transfer enough Wake for a fresh analyst or model to reproduce the useful decomposition. Any excess of live originating traversal over competent replay is a separate human-AI/path-dependence question and should be measured separately rather than credited to TDR itself.

## 13. Discussion

The simplest interpretation of TDR is modest: it is a disciplined way to ask whether a scientific representation is failing because it has silently grouped together decision-bearing distinctions.

That modest interpretation is already useful because modern science routinely moves between scales, abstractions, learned representations, effective descriptions, and heterogeneous data. In such settings, model failure can be misread in both directions. Researchers may invent new mechanism where a missing state variable would suffice, or may insist on a coarse representation after its future-sufficiency has broken. TDR inserts a diagnostic step between those extremes.

The stronger interpretation is more ambitious: a repeated, typed decomposition-reconstruction protocol may itself function as a **generator of useful scientific distinctions**. That claim cannot be established by the beauty of retrospective examples. It requires prospective comparison against serious alternatives.

This distinction between local merit and parent-generator merit is essential. TDR can fail as a general generator while individual constructs remain useful. Conversely, a positive method-level result would not validate unrelated physical or metaphysical claims in the research programme from which TDR emerged.

The proposed benchmark is therefore intentionally severe. The strongest fair baseline may win. A result in which standard expert analysis produces the same distinction faster and more reliably is a scientifically valuable reduction. It tells us that the local insight belongs to the native field rather than to a new general generator.

The paper also exposes a more general design principle for adaptive research systems: **learning from failure and being confirmed by failure are different relations**. A failed branch may generate the next hypothesis, but the descendant must face fresh evidence. The complete branch lineage remains part of any claim about generator quality. This prevents a flexible architecture from becoming unfalsifiable through endless repair.

Finally, the Wake construct makes explicit a problem that affects research communication and human-AI systems without requiring a novel ontology. The same visible state can be insufficient when different histories change the target-relevant future or admissible continuation. But history should not be retained ceremonially. It earns load only when protected testing shows that it carries information unavailable from a simpler present address.

## 14. Conclusion

Typed Decomposition-Reconstruction is proposed as a falsifiable methodological protocol, not a foundational law.

Its kernel is:

$$
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
&\text{detect target-relevant residual}\\
&\rightarrow\text{decompose the smallest decision-bearing object}\\
&\rightarrow\text{type objects, relations, boundaries, histories, and properties}\\
&\rightarrow\text{forbid unlicensed property inheritance}\\
&\rightarrow\text{reconstruct only through explicit carriers/realisations}\\
&\rightarrow\text{subtract native and simpler ownership}\\
&\rightarrow\text{test prospectively}\\
&\rightarrow\text{preserve, reopen, repair, reduce, or reject}.
\end{aligned}
}
$$

The strongest methods claim is represented by

$$
\boxed{
G_{\mathrm{gen}}(q)
=
F_q(\mathcal G_{\mathrm{TDR}})
-
F_{\mathrm{base}}^{*}(q).
}
$$

This equation is intentionally capable of returning a negative number.

If TDR cannot beat strong native and generic alternatives on fresh tasks under matched resources, the broad generator claim should contract. If it produces stable, independently reproducible, prospectively useful distinctions across multiple domains without smuggling mechanisms between them, then the result would justify a stronger methods programme.

The next scientific step is therefore not another retrospective example. It is a frozen prospective benchmark.
