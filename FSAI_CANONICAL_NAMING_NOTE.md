The note canonically names two objects already present in the public AAF/FSSR construction:

- **Future-Sufficient Address Invariant (FSAI):** a declared Address is sufficient only while histories it treats as the same remain equivalent across the declared admissible future.
- **Layer-Before-Law Precedence Principle (LBLP):** when predictive closure fails, state/address adequacy and minimal lawful readdressing are tested before the residual is promoted into evidence for revision of the governing law/model.

FSSR remains the experimental assay that attacks the state-equivalence claim with a bounded lawful future, confirms any split independently, tests the smallest physically typed repair by held-out remove/restore, and compares recruitment with a conventional mechanism marker.

## Transition-carriage corollary

The same construction has an immediate traversal consequence already implicit in AAF's recursive readdressing:

```math
A_t
\rightarrow
\mathcal F_t^+
\rightarrow
\tau_t
\rightarrow
A_{t+1}
\rightarrow
\mathcal F_{t+1}^+.
```

A realised transition may change the effective Address and therefore the admissible future. A continuation projected at $A_t$ retains standing after the transition only where its remaining suffix is still admissible from the readdressed state:

```math
\gamma_{t,\mathrm{rem}}^{\mathrm{proj}}
\in
\mathcal F^+(A_{t+1}).
```

Where that condition fails, continuation is recomputed from $A_{t+1}$ rather than inherited from the earlier projection. The state/history carrier need preserve only those distinctions whose removal would merge histories with materially different declared futures; unlimited history is not implied.

This is an explicit corollary of the existing future-sufficiency and recursive-readdressing construction, not a third named principle, a new physical law, or a claim that precomputed trajectories are generally invalid. In regimes where the transition leaves the relevant Address and future geometry unchanged, the corollary is null and the projected continuation remains admissible.
