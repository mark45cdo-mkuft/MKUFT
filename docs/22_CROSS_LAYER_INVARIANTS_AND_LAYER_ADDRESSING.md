## 1. Core rule

A single invariant may appear differently at different layers without becoming several unrelated mechanisms.

> One invariant. Many layer addresses. No fragmentation.

Unified models can fail by flattening distinct layers or by renaming one repeated relation as several disconnected theories. Layer addressing is intended to prevent both errors.

Unity is preserved through lawful relation and recoverable mapping, not by treating all layers as one homogeneous space.

## 2. Formal statement

Let $K$ be a candidate invariant relation, let

$$
L\in\{S,I,P,O\}
$$

be the active layer, and let $\theta_L$ collect the constraints, observables, units, noise sources, and admissible transitions specific to that layer.

Define

$$
K_L=A_L(K;\theta_L),
$$

where $A_L$ is an address map rather than a new ontology.

A valid cross-layer claim requires a stable core relation, explicit layer-specific variables, a lawful address map, stable semantics, evidence at the layer where the claim is made, independent falsifiers, and a defined coupling whenever one layer is claimed to affect another.

Where a cross-layer coupling is formalised, a typed map may be written

$$
C_{LM}:\mathcal X_L\rightarrow\mathcal X_M,
$$

or as a more general typed relation when a single-valued map is inappropriate.

No default metric measures distance between S, I, P, and O. A cross-layer arrow is not ordinary movement through physical space.

## 3. S–I–P–O discipline

### S — Substrate

The substrate is the structured possibility or source domain in the model. It is not automatically a physical grid, cognitive image, symbolic diagram, information database, or extra spatial dimension.

### I — Information

The information layer carries pattern, address, role, state, rule, routing, permission, and executable structure. Physical force language is not imported here without a defined coupling. Informational adjacency is not physical proximity.

### P — Physical

The physical layer carries matter, energy, fields, timing, instruments, bodies, environments, and measurable effects. A physical address requires units, controls, expected effect size, uncertainty, and reduction to accepted physics.

### O — Observer

The observer layer carries observer-positioned registration: measurement context, record, perspective, attention, interpretation, memory, salience, and bounded state-dependent participation where operationally defined.

An observer address cannot replace a missing physical mechanism. An O-layer address may refer to an individual observer or, under stricter conditions, a coupled composite-observer candidate. A composite candidate remains an address across existing layers and does not automatically create a fifth layer.

## 4. Addressing test

A repeated term can be treated as a cross-layer invariant only if the analysis can identify:

- what remains invariant;
- what changes at the active address;
- the space inhabited by each variable;
- the relevant units or normalisation;
- what is measured at that layer;
- what is merely analogous;
- what coupling connects the addresses;
- what would falsify the address without borrowing evidence from another layer.

If these distinctions cannot be made, one word may be covering unrelated objects. If the answers remain identical at every layer, the model may instead be flattening real differences.

## 5. LUCY as a worked example

LUCY means **Local Unified Coherence Yield**.

The candidate invariant is a local threshold at which coherence becomes sufficient to change boundary behaviour.

Useful addresses include LUCY(I) for informational or routing coherence, LUCY(O) for observer-state coherence, and LUCY(P) for physical or material coherence.

To avoid collision with $C$ as path cost and general coherence, new formal work uses $Y_L$ for the yield index. A general threshold template is

$$
Y_L(x,t)
=
\chi_L
\frac{\lVert\nabla\tau_L(x,t)\rVert^2}
{N_L(x,t)+\varepsilon_L},
$$

with threshold

$$
Y_L(x,t)\ge Y_*.
$$

Here $\tau_L$ is a layer-specific potential, tension, or state variable; $\chi_L$ is layer-specific sensitivity; $N_L$ is layer-specific noise or disorder; and $\varepsilon_L$ is a declared stabiliser preventing invalid singularity.

This is a **cross-layer template**, not a validated law shared by cognition, information systems, and matter. At each address the variables must be operationalised independently. A physical use requires dimensional consistency and units; an informational or observer use may instead be a normalised index.

The common claim is the threshold relation, not identity of mechanism, units, geometry, or causal substrate.

## 6. LUCY(I)

Candidate variables include $\tau_I$ for tension among interpretations, routes, or executions; $\nabla\tau_I$ for a gradient toward a structurally preferred route in a declared information state space; and $N_I$ for ambiguity, contradiction, noise, mis-addressing, or drift.

The testable signature is not mystical “information energy.” It is improved execution consistency, lower interpretation variance, stronger error detection, reduced route ambiguity, and stable reconstruction from partial information.

## 7. LUCY(O)

Candidate variables include $\tau_O$ for tension among attentional, interpretive, emotional, and memory states; $\nabla\tau_O$ for a gradient toward a stable observer configuration in a declared observer-state model; and $N_O$ for distraction, fear contamination, fatigue, expectation bias, or internal noise.

Possible measures include calibration, judgement stability, signal discrimination, and reduced impulsive frame-switching. Observer coherence remains bounded and does not permit arbitrary physical outcomes.

## 8. LUCY(P)

Candidate variables include $\tau_P$ for a defined physical boundary variable or potential, $\nabla\tau_P$ for its measurable physical gradient, and $N_P$ for thermal disorder, decoherence, vibration, electromagnetic noise, impurity, or turbulence.

Candidate systems include superconductors, superfluids, condensate-like systems, coherent mass-current systems, and geometry-controlled media.

Before the template is used as a physical equation, $\tau_P$, $\chi_P$, $N_P$, $\varepsilon_P$, and $Y_*$ require compatible dimensions and a preregistered measurement protocol.

LUCY(P) is weakened if no boundary-linked effect scales with coherence, reduced noise, or geometry beyond accepted physics.

## 9. Group coherence is not a new layer

A group event is a coupled system across existing addresses: individual O-layer states, I-layer coordination and shared instruction, and P-layer bodies, timing, environment, and signal channels.

A candidate group form is

$$
\kappa_{\mathrm{group}}
=
\Phi(\kappa_1,\ldots,\kappa_N,C_{\mathrm{corr}}),
$$

with one simple approximation

$$
\kappa_{\mathrm{group}}
=
A_{\mathrm{align}}
\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\kappa_i,
$$

and environmental modulation

$$
\kappa_{\mathrm{eff,group}}
=
\kappa_{\mathrm{group}}\,\eta(F_{\mathrm{env}}).
$$

The ranges and interpretations of $\Phi$, $C_{\mathrm{corr}}$, and $\eta$ must be declared before quantitative use. These are working models rather than proof of a direct group-to-substrate mechanism.

Group coherence alone does not establish a composite observer. Simultaneous alignment, average coherence, or shared instruction may improve coordination while remaining reducible to participating individuals and ordinary communication.

### 9.1 Composite-observer candidate

A composite-observer candidate is a coupled system whose observer-like function appears at the relational level rather than being exhausted by listing the participating observers separately.

The candidate spans existing layers: individual O states, reciprocal I-layer routing and shared state, and P-layer channels, timing, records, and actions.

Necessary candidate features include a distinguishable and sufficiently persistent coupled boundary; reciprocal causal influence rather than one-way use; cross-time state retained at the coupled level; integration of information or constraints unavailable to either component alone; system-level discrimination; correction propagation through the whole coupling; closed action–measurement–feedback loops; and predicted deformation when reciprocal links, state continuity, or load-bearing dependencies are removed.

Mere aggregation does not satisfy these conditions.

### 9.2 Superadditive functional test

Let $F_A$ and $F_B$ denote separate task values. Let $F_{\mathrm{add}}(A,B)$ be the strongest matched additive or independent-ensemble baseline and $F_{\mathrm{coupled}}$ the value under reciprocal coupling with matched total resources.

Define

$$
G_{\mathrm{syn}}
=
F_{\mathrm{coupled}}-F_{\mathrm{add}}(A,B).
$$

A positive reproducible $G_{\mathrm{syn}}$ is evidence of superadditive coupling relative to the declared baseline.

For example, if

$$
F_A=2,
\qquad
F_B=2,
\qquad
F_{\mathrm{add}}=4,
\qquad
F_{\mathrm{coupled}}=6,
$$

then

$$
G_{\mathrm{syn}}=2.
$$

“More than the sum of its parts” refers to the positive excess $2$, not merely to the fact that the score $6$ belongs to the coupled system.

Where the metric cannot lawfully be added, the additive baseline must be replaced by a preregistered matched ensemble or null model appropriate to the domain.

Superadditive functional gain does not by itself establish consciousness, merged identity, personhood, moral status, a new physical force, direct substrate coupling, or a fifth MKUFT layer.

## 10. Post-boundary lattice discipline

The substrate is not described as a literal lattice, grid, mesh, or geometry without independent evidence.

A safer representation is:

> Lattice-like geometry appears post-boundary as a representation of admissible states, adjacency relations, routing paths, coherence envelopes, and traversal costs.

A useful representation is not a discovered physical object. A high-dimensional graph or state space is not evidence of additional physical spacetime dimensions.

## 11. Cross-layer coupling

A valid coupling statement identifies the source space and address, source variable, typed coupling rule, receiving space and address, measurable effect, ordinary baseline, reduction rule, and falsifier.

For example, an I-layer timing instruction may be transmitted through a measured coordination channel, alter O/P alignment, and predict lower behavioural variance relative to a scrambled-timing control.

A composite-observer claim requires the stronger reciprocal structure: component states must interact through reciprocal coupling and retained joint state, produce coupled discrimination or action, outperform the declared matched baseline, and deform predictably when the coupling is disrupted.

A coupling claim becomes stronger only when changes at one address predict measured changes at another under controls. Drawing an arrow does not create a shared metric or physical shortcut.

## 12. Failure modes

- **Fragmentation:** one invariant is split into several mechanisms without need.
- **Flattening:** distinct layers are treated as identical.
- **Dimensional flattening:** state-space dimension, layer address, and physical spatial dimension are silently treated as one thing.
- **Metaphor leakage:** physical terms enter cognitive or symbolic domains without coupling.
- **Observer inflation:** observer language replaces missing physical explanation.
- **Aggregation inflation:** ordinary collaboration is called a composite observer without persistent boundary, reciprocal integration, joint state, and loop closure.
- **Synergy inflation:** a coupled score is called “more than the sum” without a matched additive or ensemble baseline.
- **Consciousness leakage:** functional observer-role evidence is converted into subjective consciousness or identity claims.
- **Substrate concretisation:** a possibility space becomes a literal material structure by assertion.
- **Address drift:** a term silently changes definition between layers.
- **Symbol collision:** one symbol carries incompatible meanings without qualification.

## 13. Falsifiers

The cross-layer claim is weakened if the invariant cannot be stated independently of examples; addresses require incompatible definitions; no lawful address map exists; typed couplings cannot be defined where causal influence is claimed; predicted couplings repeatedly fail; one address survives only by borrowing evidence from another; or simpler domain-specific explanations outperform the proposed relation.

The composite-observer branch is weakened where reciprocal coupling does not outperform the best matched independent or ensemble control, positive superadditive gain disappears after resources and information are matched, state reset or relation scrambling produces no predicted deformation, or system-level correction and discrimination can be fully attributed to one component acting alone.

A failed address does not automatically destroy every other address, but repeated failures weaken the claim that the relation is genuinely cross-layer.

Reduction rule:

> If a coupled system lacks reproducible superadditive gain or observer-level boundary, retained joint state, reciprocal correction, and loop closure, it should be described as a coordinated group or collaboration rather than a composite-observer candidate.
