## 1. Core refinement

A lower-scale degree of freedom need not be physically destroyed when a higher-scale organisation forms. The stronger and more testable possibility is that the lower-scale variable remains physically instantiated while losing **independent relational addressability** for the lower-scale individuality that previously possessed it.

The distinction is therefore:

1. **physical persistence** — the underlying variable or invariant still exists in the realised substrate;
2. **independent addressability** — the current organised object can still vary, recruit, or route that variable as an independently available relation to itself;
3. **scale ownership** — the variable may remain causally active while becoming bound into a higher-scale constraint relation rather than remaining a free coordinate of the lower-scale individuality.

This prevents the phrase `loss of degrees of freedom` from being misread as literal disappearance.

> The invariant can persist while its independent address changes.

## 2. Lower-scale variables and higher-scale effective state

Let a lower-scale physical state be

$$
x\in\mathcal X_\ell,
$$

and let

$$
I_i:\mathcal X_\ell\rightarrow\mathcal Y_i
$$

be a declared lower-scale variable, invariant, or relation-coordinate.

Let a higher-scale effective description be produced by a declared map

$$
q_{\ell\rightarrow L}:\mathcal X_\ell\rightarrow\mathcal X_L.
$$

Then distinct lower-scale states can be equivalent with respect to the chosen higher-scale description:

$$
x\sim_L y
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
q_{\ell\rightarrow L}(x)=q_{\ell\rightarrow L}(y).
$$

The existence of such an effective map is not itself novel; coarse-graining, effective variables, and quotient-like descriptions are established mathematical and physical tools. The MKUFT question is narrower: **does the organised system itself lose independent access to lower-scale relations as a new higher-scale closure becomes load-bearing?**

## 3. Identity-preserving reachable set

Let

$$
\mathcal V_L\subseteq\mathcal X_\ell
$$

be the lower-realisation set compatible with persistence of the declared higher-scale identity or viable organisational class.

Define the identity-preserving reachable set

$$
\mathcal R^{\mathrm{id}}_{L,t}(x)
=
\left\{
 x'\in\mathcal R_t(x):
 x'\in\mathcal V_L
\right\}.
$$

This set contains states reachable without leaving the higher-scale identity class under the declared viability and organisation conditions.

A lower-scale variable may remain physically present while becoming impossible to vary independently inside this set.

## 4. Independent relational addressability

Let

$$
\mathcal A_\ell(t)=\{I_1,I_2,\ldots,I_n\}
$$

be the declared lower-scale relations that were independently addressable before consolidation.

For a tolerance pair $(\varepsilon_i,\delta_L)$, call $I_i$ **independently addressable under the higher-scale organisation** when there exist

$$
x',x''\in\mathcal R^{\mathrm{id}}_{L,t}(x)
$$

such that

$$
\left\lVert I_i(x')-I_i(x'')\right\rVert>\varepsilon_i,
$$

while the other declared higher-scale state variables required to remain fixed for the test vary by no more than $\delta_L$.

Define the surviving independently addressable set

$$
\mathcal A_{\ell\mid L}(t)
=
\left\{
I_i\in\mathcal A_\ell(t):
I_i\text{ passes the identity-preserving addressability test}
\right\}.
$$

A loss of lower-scale independent addressability is then

$$
\Delta A_\ell
=
\mu_A\!\left(\mathcal A_{\ell\mid L}(t_2)\right)
-
\mu_A\!\left(\mathcal A_{\ell}(t_1)\right)
<0,
$$

where $\mu_A$ is a declared count or measure appropriate to the system.

This is not a statement that the corresponding molecules, forces, variables, or physical relations have vanished. It is a statement about **which relations remain independently usable without breaking the new organisation**.

## 5. Constraint binding rather than destruction

Suppose a lower-scale action or movement set before consolidation is

$$
\mathcal U_{\ell}^{\mathrm{self}}(x).
$$

After incorporation into a higher-scale organisation, the identity-preserving subset may be

$$
\mathcal U_{\ell\mid L}^{\mathrm{self}}(x)
=
\mathcal U_{\ell}^{\mathrm{self}}(x)
\cap
\mathcal U_{L}^{\mathrm{adm}}(x),
$$

with

$$
\mathcal U_{\ell\mid L}^{\mathrm{self}}(x)
\subsetneq
\mathcal U_{\ell}^{\mathrm{self}}(x).
$$

The excluded routes are not necessarily physically impossible in isolation. They are inaccessible **while remaining this higher-scale organised object**.

This is the proposed operational meaning of a lower-scale individuality losing old degrees of freedom as relations to itself while the underlying physical capacities remain latent in the realised substrate.

## 6. Scale-transition onset and completion

A scale transition should not be defined only by the later appearance of a new capability. The earliest candidate onset is the first sustained point at which lower-scale independent addressability is lost because the new relation has become load-bearing.

Define a provisional onset time

$$
t_{\mathrm{access}}
=
\inf\left\{
t:
\Delta A_\ell(t)<-\eta_A
\text{ and }
\Gamma_L(t)\ge\tau_\Gamma
\right\},
$$

where $\Gamma_L$ is a declared measure of higher-scale closure or organisational persistence and $\eta_A,\tau_\Gamma$ are preregistered thresholds.

A fuller scale transition is established only when new higher-scale addressability or capability is also present:

$$
\Delta A_\ell<0,
\qquad
\Delta A_L>0,
\qquad
\Delta K_L>0,
$$

under lawful comparison rules at each scale.

This separates two questions:

- **onset:** when did the old individuality lose independent access to previously available relations?
- **completion:** when did that binding support stable new higher-scale relations or capabilities?

The transition may be sharp or extended. A biological developmental process need not cross at one instantaneous clock tick.

## 7. Address transfer

The strongest version of the proposal is not

```text
lower invariant disappears
→ higher property appears
```

but

```text
lower invariant persists
→ independent lower-scale addressability decreases
→ the invariant becomes bound into a higher-scale relation
→ new higher-scale coordinates or capabilities become addressable
```

This can be written schematically as

$$
I_i\text{ persists},
\qquad
A_i^{(\ell)}\downarrow,
\qquad
A_j^{(L)}\uparrow.
$$

`Address transfer` is a descriptive term for this change in relational availability. It does not imply that a physical quantity literally migrates between mathematical spaces.

## 8. Distinguishing coarse-graining from endogenous loss of access

Ordinary coarse-graining can remove fine detail from **our model** while leaving the real system unchanged. This supplement therefore requires a stronger test than successful macroscopic compression.

A claimed endogenous scale transition must show that interventionally accessible routes of the lower-scale unit are actually reduced by the higher-scale organisation, not merely omitted by the observer's description.

Useful controls include:

1. **observer-only coarse-graining control:** change the model resolution without changing the physical coupling; lower-scale behavioural access should remain unchanged;
2. **constraint-release control:** remove the proposed higher-scale binding while preserving the lower machinery; predicted lower-scale autonomy should return where reversible;
3. **closure-preserving perturbation:** vary the lower variable only within the higher-scale viability class and measure whether independent control remains possible;
4. **component-preservation control:** verify that the physical machinery underlying the old capability remains present even when its independent deployment is suppressed;
5. **higher-scale gain control:** show that lost lower-scale access is associated with a separately measured higher-scale capability rather than mere suppression.

## 9. Example test geometry — motile cells to organised collective

For a motile lower-scale unit, a free heading or movement repertoire can remain physically supported by cytoskeleton, motors, receptors, and energy supply while adhesion, signalling, polarity, and collective geometry restrict which trajectories are independently available.

The key measurement is therefore not only whether movement variance falls. Measure whether:

- the machinery for the old trajectory class is still present;
- release from the organising relation restores part of the old trajectory repertoire;
- restriction occurs selectively while higher-scale coordinated movement improves;
- a new collective coordinate, such as coherent group direction or centre-of-mass navigation, becomes controllable or predictively useful.

This yields the desired pattern:

$$
\text{physical persistence of lower machinery}
\;\land\;
\Delta A_\ell<0
\;\land\;
\Delta K_L>0.
$$

That pattern supports **incorporation** rather than destruction.

## 10. Relationship to established neighbours

This refinement sits near several established ideas and does not claim them as inventions.

- Coarse-graining and multiscale modelling routinely integrate out or suppress fine degrees of freedom in effective descriptions while retaining the underlying microphysical system.
- Renormalisation-group methods identify scale-relevant variables and discard irrelevant detail for a chosen effective description.
- Motor-control work on controlled and uncontrolled manifolds gives operational meaning to which degrees of freedom are stabilised for a task and which remain variable.
- Organisational-closure theory distinguishes constraints that mutually maintain an organised biological system and can help mark system boundaries.

The present MKUFT refinement is the **integration of physical persistence, endogenous independent addressability, load-bearing closure, addressed state comparison, and scale-separated capability into one scale-transition test**. Historical priority for that conjunction is not asserted here.

## 11. Failure and reduction conditions

This refinement is weakened if:

- the lower-scale capability disappears because its physical machinery is destroyed rather than relationally bound;
- the apparent loss of access occurs only in the observer's coarse-grained model;
- release of the proposed organising constraint does not restore lower-scale autonomy where reversibility is predicted;
- no stable higher-scale closure or capability appears;
- the claimed higher-scale object adds no predictive or interventional value beyond independent lower-scale components;
- the result depends on arbitrary post-hoc choices of addressability threshold or macrostate definition.

If those failures occur, use the simpler physical or coarse-grained description instead.

## 12. Claim boundary

This module does **not** establish that every emergence event is a scale transition of this form. It does not establish a universal sharp transition time. It does not establish consciousness, survival after death, reincarnation, `source`, or independent I→P dynamics.

Those are higher-layer hypotheses and must not be inferred merely because lower-scale incorporation and address loss can be formalised physically.

The scientifically useful claim is narrower:

> A candidate scale transition can be tested as a change in **independent relational accessibility** rather than literal disappearance of lower-scale invariants: the lower physical variables can persist while higher-scale closure removes their independent deployment and makes new higher-scale relations or capabilities accessible.

## 13. Compressed rule

> **Do not ask only whether the old degree of freedom still exists. Ask whether the old individuality can still address it independently while remaining the same organised object.**

> **A scale transition begins when previously independent lower-scale relations become load-bearingly bound; it is completed when that loss supports stable new higher-scale addressability or capability.**
