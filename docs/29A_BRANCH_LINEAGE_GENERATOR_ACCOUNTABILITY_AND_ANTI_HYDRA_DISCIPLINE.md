**Author:** Mark Charles McLaughlin  
**Framework:** McLaughlin–Kairos Unified Field Theory (MKUFT)

**Status:** standing scientific claim-accountability and branch-lineage control. It introduces no new equation, physical mechanism, ontology, or empirical result.

## 1. Purpose

A broad adaptive research framework can become difficult to falsify even when each individual branch has a local failure condition.

The failure mode is:

```text
branch predicts X
→ X fails
→ branch is cut away or reinterpreted as exploratory
→ a new branch is generated from the failure
→ the new branch is tested
→ repeat until one branch survives
→ surviving branch is presented as evidence that the parent engine works
```

That is not adequate scientific hygiene.

MKUFT therefore separates two distinct questions:

1. **What does this local result prove or falsify?**
2. **What does the complete history of generated branches say about the parent framework as a hypothesis-generating, discriminating, or search architecture?**

The first is controlled by typed dependency and claim scope. The second is controlled by lineage accounting.

> **Locality governs what a result proves. Lineage governs what the attempt costs.**

## 2. Two propagation channels

A branch result can affect a parent in two different ways. They must not be confused.

### 2.1 Proposition / dependency propagation

A local success or failure updates a wider scientific claim only when the tested proposition is a declared consequence of that wider claim at the relevant strength.

Before result inspection, classify the branch relation as one of:

- **exploratory candidate** — generated by the architecture but not required by the parent;
- **predicted consequence** — the parent architecture materially predicts the outcome;
- **necessary consequence** — the wider claim requires the outcome within the declared conditions;
- **independent neighbouring branch** — scientifically related by provenance or method but not evidentially dependent.

A local result may not be promoted or demoted after the fact by changing this relationship merely because the result was convenient or inconvenient.

### 2.2 Performance / lineage propagation

Every serious generated branch enters the parent architecture's performance history when the parent is later credited as a generator of scientific hypotheses, discriminators, methods, or cross-domain insight.

This remains true even when the branch was exploratory and therefore does not directly falsify a parent proposition.

A failed exploratory branch may have little or no proposition-level force against another branch, while still counting as a failed attempt in any claim about the productivity, efficiency, selectivity, or reliability of the parent generator.

> **Pruning a failed branch does not erase it from the generator's record.**

## 3. Local merit is preserved

The anti-hydra rule must not create the opposite error of guilt by association.

If an ATLD result independently survives its own matched controls, later failure of a biological candidate does not erase the ATLD result.

If a Bell correspondence or exact geometric map is mathematically valid, a failure in another application does not retroactively invalidate that local result.

Likewise, success in one branch does not validate a neighbouring branch, an S-layer physical ontology, or foundational MKUFT physics.

Use:

```text
local truth / local null / local failure
→ preserved at its tested address
```

and separately:

```text
complete branch lineage
→ contributes to evaluation of the parent generator
```

This protects both merit and falsifiability.

## 4. Failed branches remain in the denominator

When a broader claim is made about MKUFT as a scientific reasoning, search, hypothesis-generation, or cross-domain discovery architecture, do not report only surviving branches.

Preserve at least where recoverable:

- serious candidate branches generated;
- which were exploratory, predicted, or necessary consequences;
- nulls and failures;
- branches that collapsed into established prior art;
- branches withdrawn for mathematical or conceptual defects;
- branches that survived prospectively;
- search time, compute, human input, and information access where comparison depends on them;
- repairs or coordinate additions introduced after earlier failures;
- dependencies among branches so correlated descendants are not counted as independent successes.

A useful positive result is not diluted merely because failed attempts exist. The point is that the denominator must remain visible whenever generator-level merit is claimed.

> **The parent is judged on the portfolio when the parent claims credit for producing the portfolio.**

## 5. Descendant repairs inherit ancestry

A failed test may legitimately expose a missing variable, better discriminator, improved state description, or stronger ordinary null.

Scientific learning from failure is allowed and desirable.

But the lineage must remain explicit:

```text
Prediction 1 fails
→ failure exposes candidate missing distinction
→ Prediction 2 is generated
→ Prediction 2 faces fresh evidence
```

Do not use:

```text
Prediction 1 fails
→ repair explains Prediction 1 retrospectively
→ repair is counted as confirmed by the same data
```

The governing rule is:

> **A failure may generate the next hypothesis. It may not validate it.**

Prediction 2 may earn full local credit if it succeeds prospectively. Its success is then typed as a successful descendant repair or new prediction, not as an independent pre-existing confirmation of the original branch.

## 6. Fresh-evidence rule

A repair, new coordinate, narrowed boundary, changed scale, new history variable, or readdressed mechanism introduced because of a failed result requires evidence not already consumed in generating that repair.

Preferred routes include:

- held-out data;
- a preregistered follow-up;
- a new experiment;
- an independent corpus;
- a new model family or evaluator;
- another domain where the mapping was fixed before the result was known.

If no fresh discriminator is available, the repair remains a post-hoc explanatory candidate.

## 7. Complexity and flexibility carry a cost

A framework that can add a new scale, history variable, boundary, receiver, relation, address, or hidden-state coordinate after every miss has more representational freedom than a simpler competitor.

That flexibility is not free scientific credit.

A newly recruited distinction earns retention when it improves prospective discrimination enough to justify the additional freedom it introduces.

Depending on the domain, legitimate controls may include:

- held-out predictive performance;
- preregistered ablation/restoration;
- model-complexity penalties;
- Bayesian model comparison;
- minimum-description-length style comparison;
- corrected multiple testing;
- matched search budgets;
- explicit accounting of additional human/model search effort.

No one penalty is universal. The invariant is:

> **Retrospective fit is not enough to pay for added flexibility.**

## 8. Parent-level generator test

If MKUFT, Layer Before Law, ATLD-derived machinery, or another parent object is claimed to be a broadly useful scientific generator rather than merely a collection of individually testable ideas, the generator itself requires a finite external discriminator.

Where practicable, freeze before outcome inspection:

- task or problem family;
- admissible information;
- branch/hypothesis or search budget;
- time and compute;
- human assistance and revision opportunity;
- strong comparator methods;
- scoring and novelty rules;
- prior-art subtraction procedure;
- success, null, and failure criteria;
- stopping condition.

Then evaluate the whole prospective portfolio rather than selecting its best survivors.

A broad methodological claim is strengthened if the architecture produces better prospective hypotheses, discriminators, corrections, hidden-state discoveries, or model choices than strong alternatives under a fair search envelope.

It contracts if it does not.

A local scientific result that independently survives remains locally valid even if the parent generator fails this broader performance test.

## 9. No post-hoc scope escape

After an adequately specified prospective test fails, do not rescue it by introducing a previously unmentioned coordinate and treating the original result as success.

The lawful sequence is:

```text
original claim / test
→ FAIL or NULL at its frozen scope
→ record the result
→ generate repair hypothesis if warranted
→ assign a new version / prediction / branch
→ test prospectively
```

The original miss remains on the lineage ledger.

Likewise, a parent may not declare after the result that the tested branch was never representative if the branch was previously used as evidence of the parent's breadth, predictive reach, or generative power.

## 10. Cross-domain credit and damage

Cross-domain transfer creates two symmetrical risks:

- **halo:** one successful application is used to validate the whole architecture;
- **amputation immunity:** each failed application is cut away so the parent never accumulates evidential cost.

The control is not automatic global propagation in either direction.

Use both channels:

```text
proposition dependency
→ determines what the local result logically supports or falsifies

lineage accounting
→ determines what the attempt contributes to the generator-level record
```

This allows a biology null to remain a biology null while still counting honestly in any later claim that the architecture systematically generates high-value cross-domain tests.

## 11. Independence and correlated descendants

Several related branches generated from the same observation, prompt, failed experiment, prior-art seam, or shared model prior are not automatically several independent confirmations.

When generator-level evidence is scored, identify common-cause blocks and descendant relations.

Do not use:

```text
five repaired descendants survive
→ five independent confirmations
```

when all five inherited the same discovery event or information source.

Count the joint lineage at the scale justified by the dependency structure.

## 12. Failure conditions of this discipline

This control fails if it is used to:

- erase a valid local result merely because the parent generator performs poorly elsewhere;
- make every exploratory miss count as falsification of foundational physics;
- classify a branch as exploratory only after it fails;
- hide dead branches from a generator-level success claim;
- allow repaired descendants to reset the attempt count;
- use the same data both to invent and confirm a repair;
- grow unlimited coordinates without prospective complexity cost;
- choose a favourable subset of tasks after results are known;
- compare the architecture with deliberately weak alternative generators;
- treat internal recursion, elegance, or self-audit as independent evidence of generator superiority;
- make portfolio accounting so broad that no bounded branch can ever earn local closure.

## 13. Scientific compression

The standing rule is:

> **Locality governs what a result proves. Lineage governs what the attempt costs. Preserve local merit and local failure at their tested addresses. A failed branch may be pruned from the live theory, but it remains in the parent generator's performance history. A repair may be learned from failure, but it requires fresh evidence and does not rewrite the original miss. When the parent claims credit as a scientific generator, judge it on the complete prospective portfolio under a fair search budget and strong alternatives, not only on surviving limbs.**

A shorter operational form is:

> **A framework may learn from its failures. It may not use the ability to grow another branch as evidence that it survived the test.**
