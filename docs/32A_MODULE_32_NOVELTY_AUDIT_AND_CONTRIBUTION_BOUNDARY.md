## 1. Purpose

Module 32 began from an independently reached chain about recursive learning, self-maintained constraint, changing future reach, and scale-dependent freedom/capability. Same-day refinement then added load-bearing-set tests, relation-versus-carrier substitution, relational addressability, same-self parity, temporal-continuity kernels, typed relation scopes, completion fibers, quotient-based scale readout, macrostate realisation classes, temporal mismatch exposure, an I→P transition-support assay, and finally an intrinsic/extrinsic address split with path transport, loop-history tests, context-indexed P-realisation fibers, and boundary-conditioned physical comparators.

A literature audit found substantial prior and adjacent work. The scientific task is therefore not to defend every ingredient as new. It is to identify what prior work already supplies, what the current MKUFT integration adds operationally, and what remains only a candidate contribution pending broader review and empirical use.

The governing rules are:

> **Independent convergence can support confidence in a reasoning route, but scientific novelty belongs only to the residual structure not already supplied by prior work.**

> **A new canon write is a changed object. Re-audit the integrated object; if the write exposes another load-bearing relation or contradiction, continue refinement rather than treating the successful commit as closure.**

The second rule is process provenance, not a novelty claim about scientific method.

## 2. Strong prior art that removes broad novelty claims

### 2.1 Constraint closure and autonomous organisation

Autopoiesis, autonomy/enaction, and closure-of-constraints work already treat living organisation as relational, self-maintaining, boundary-dependent, historically structured, and coupled to its environment. Montévil and Mossio (2015) explicitly formalise biological organisation as closure of constraints.

**Boundary:** MKUFT does not claim invention of constraint closure, autopoiesis, relational individuality, viability, self-maintenance, or organism–environment coupling.

### 2.2 Changing possibility spaces and accessible-state topology

Longo, Montévil and Kauffman (2012), Kauffman and Roli (2021), and Adams et al. (2024) develop changing biological possibility spaces, internally maintained constraints, accessible-state structure, and emergent/local rule change.

**Boundary:** MKUFT does not claim invention of changing phase/possibility spaces, enablement, internally maintained constraints, or the broad claim that organisation can alter accessible states.

### 2.3 Semantic/symbol–matter control

Pattee and related biosemiotic work already study symbol–matter, semantic closure, coding, measurement, and physical constraint.

**Boundary:** MKUFT does not claim invention of semantic closure or the general claim that informational organisation can be physically realised through constraints.

### 2.4 Ablation, multiple realisation, coarse-graining, fibers and quotients

Ablation, necessary-condition tests, alternative physical realisation, quotient descriptions, fibers/preimages, equivalence classes, coarse-graining, macrovariables, and multiple realisability are established mathematical and scientific methods.

Hoel, Albantakis and Tononi (2013) explicitly formalise cases in which coarse-grained macrostates have many microstate realisations and can exhibit greater effective causal information than the microscopic description.

**Boundary:** 32S/32S3 do not claim invention of these ingredients or the general idea that one macrostate can have many microstate realisations.

### 2.5 Local-to-global mathematics and contextual covers

Nerve constructions, sheaves, restriction/gluing, and local-to-global consistency are established mathematics. Robinson (2018) develops approximate consistency through a consistency radius for sheaves of pseudometric spaces. Abramsky and Brandenburger (2011) use sheaf theory over measurement contexts to formulate non-locality and contextuality.

**Boundary:** 32S3 does not claim invention of nerve complexes, sheaves, consistency residuals, contextual covers, gluing, or local-to-global obstruction.

The Abramsky–Brandenburger case is also a useful hygiene reminder: a `local` context in local-to-global mathematics need not mean a literal spatially adjacent patch.

### 2.6 Hypergraphs and higher-order interactions

Hypergraphs, simplicial complexes, and higher-order networks are established tools for relations involving more than two components. Zhang, Lucas and Battiston (2023) show that the chosen higher-order representation can itself change collective dynamical predictions.

**Boundary:** MKUFT does not claim invention of arbitrary higher-order relation scopes or hypergraph-style representation. 32S3 uses typed relation scopes because a purely geometric nerve would be too restrictive for the framework's own stated relation classes.

### 2.7 Causal semantic information

Kolchinsky and Wolpert (2018) define semantic information using counterfactual scrambling and causal contribution to system persistence.

**Boundary:** 32S3 does not claim invention of intervention-based semantics, causal scrambling, or the idea that meaning can be operationalised by consequence.

### 2.8 Information conversion, synergy, and causal emergence

Causal-emergence and partial-information work already formalise changes in informational organisation across scales. Varley and Hoel (2022) show coarse-graining can convert redundant, unique, and synergistic information, including increased synergy at a macroscale.

**Boundary:** MKUFT does not claim invention of macro-scale synergy or the broad idea that a macrodescription may expose information structure obscured at a microdescription.

### 2.9 Holographic phase encoding and reconstruction

Optical and metasurface holography are established physics. Huang et al. (2013) demonstrate 3D holographic reconstruction with subwavelength elements whose orientations encode phase.

**Boundary:** holography is a physical comparator and deformation template only. MKUFT does not claim invention of phase encoding, Fourier/Fresnel reconstruction, distributed optical information, or a universal holographic principle for biology or reality.

### 2.10 P-layer biological comparators remain P-layer evidence

Guruciaga et al. (2026) show boundary geometry controlling topological-defect configuration and lumen nucleation location in mouse embryonic tissue. Schone et al. (2025) show stable cortical hand/finger representations after arm amputation.

**Boundary:** these are useful P-layer comparators for relational address, boundary-conditioned interior structure, and persistence after endpoint removal. Neither establishes an independent I layer or I→P mechanism.

### 2.11 Connections, parallel transport, holonomy, and geometric phase are prior work

Fiber bundles, connections, parallel transport, and holonomy are standard differential geometry and mathematical physics. Simon (1983) identified Berry's geometric phase with holonomy in a Hermitian line bundle, and Berry (1984) derived the path-dependent geometric phase acquired by an adiabatically transported quantum eigenstate.

**Boundary:** 32S4 does not claim invention of intrinsic/extrinsic decomposition, parallel transport, holonomy, geometric phase, or the fact that equal endpoints can retain path-dependent state information in systems where the relevant connection exists.

Its use is methodological: make path transport explicit inside the existing addressed-state, identity-history, and admissibility architecture.

### 2.12 Boundary-conditioned field spectra, Casimir geometry, and driven vacuum amplification are prior work

Boundary-value problems, cavity modes, Casimir interactions, fluctuation-induced forces, and the dynamical Casimir effect are established physics. Nanostructured Casimir experiments show measurable geometry/material dependence; Wilson et al. (2011) demonstrated real photon generation under rapid driven modulation of an effective electromagnetic boundary.

Jaffe (2005) also provides an important interpretive guard: Casimir effects need not be treated as proof of a literal extractable zero-point-energy reservoir.

**Boundary:** 32S4 does not claim invention of boundary-conditioned spectra, Casimir geometry, dynamical Casimir photon generation, vacuum fluctuations, or quantum passivity. These are P-layer comparators and energy-accounting constraints, not evidence for independent I→P dynamics or free energy.

## 5. Why the scale-separated assay matters

The intuition that restriction of components can enable a larger whole is not new. The testable refinement is to keep the observables separate.

Where before/after comparison is lawful:

$$
\Delta\mu_\ell<0,
\qquad
\Delta\nu_L>0
$$

is an enabling-constraint pattern, while

$$
\Delta\mu_\ell<0,
\qquad
\Delta\nu_L<0
$$

is an overconstraint control.

Restriction is not itself progress. Higher-scale capability has to be demonstrated separately.

## 6. Why the address, identity, completion, realisation, and transport guards matter

The Module 32 family now carries five distinct anti-flattening rules.

1. **Changing construction address:** if the active state space changes, direct subtraction/equality is not licensed without a translation, embedding, quotient, or common observable.
2. **Changing individuality address:** if constitutive boundary, environment coupling, maintenance or identity-equivalence rules change, `the same self lost freedom` is not licensed without a continuity map/test.
3. **Path-dependent individuality:** if identity depends on recent trajectory, endpoint similarity is insufficient; the history must be transported and its minimum load-bearing horizon tested.
4. **Missing or coarse-grained structure:** absence is not zero structure and a quotient is not automatically a new physical object. Completion must be computed from the relations touching the missing address, while macro readout persistence is represented by a realisation class and still requires separate effective-object promotion.
5. **Intrinsic versus extrinsic address:** preserved internal relation does not imply unchanged contextual embedding, and changed context does not automatically destroy the internal relation class. When the path through context can be load-bearing, same endpoint is insufficient; compare transport/loop history. Compatible P-realisation remains distinct from actual occupancy.

These rules do not assert that environment, history, relation scopes, completion fibers, transport connections, boundary modes or I-layer variables are always constitutive. Each must earn load-bearing status in the claim being tested.

## 11. Current verdict

The expanded audit narrows the Module 32 family but does not reduce it to rediscovery. The mathematics of fibers, quotients, hypergraphs, sheaves, coarse-graining, multiple realisation, transport and holonomy is prior art; boundary-conditioned field physics and holography are prior physical comparators.

The strongest defensible residual is the **integration**: an addressed, scale-separated, deformation-tested framework in which typed relations define admissibility; absence is represented by a conditional completion fiber; lower-level turnover can be tested inside a macrostate realisation class; scale promotion is separated from observer coarse-graining; intrinsic relation is separated from extrinsic contextual address; path transport is tested when endpoint equality can hide history; compatible P-realisation is separated from actual occupancy; identity continuity is separately guarded; mismatch is separated from cost; an I→P claim must survive an adequate addressed physical-state/history null and causal-identification discipline; and boundary/vacuum analogies cannot bypass ordinary field theory or energy accounting.

That residual is significant enough to preserve canonically and investigate empirically, while remaining open to correction by earlier literature, stronger comparison models, or failed experiments.
