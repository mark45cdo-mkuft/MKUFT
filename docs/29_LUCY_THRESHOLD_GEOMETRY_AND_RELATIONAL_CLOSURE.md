## Abstract

General relativity already removes gravity from the ordinary Newtonian category of a force, describing free motion through spacetime geometry. A separate body of research treats spacetime and gravitational dynamics as emergent, induced, thermodynamic, hydrodynamic, or collective phenomena. This module therefore does not claim novelty for emergent gravity itself.

It develops a narrower MKUFT extension. Local Unified Coherence Yield (LUCY) is defined as the threshold at which a local relational configuration becomes self-consistent enough to support a stable boundary, followed by a membrane regime (LUCY-1) and a sustained pocket or domain regime (LUCY-2). The central hypothesis is that some gravity-like observations may be effective outputs of a selected relational geometry rather than evidence of a separately generated wave, particle, or force.

A conventional Landau-Ginzburg and scalar-tensor style scaffold is used only as a toy realisation. Neutron-rich superheavy nuclei are treated as one candidate class of extreme configuration-sensitive matter because shell effects can stabilise nuclei that would otherwise fission rapidly and astrophysical r-process environments can access nuclei far beyond current terrestrial synthesis. No established result connects such nuclei to controllable gravitational behaviour. The cross-layer coupling may be zero.

The module states prior art, recovery requirements, discriminating signatures, evidence tiers, and falsifiers.

## 1. Scientific position

Two points are not novel:

1. In general relativity, gravitation is not an ordinary force transmitted through a fixed background. Freely falling bodies follow geodesics of a dynamical spacetime geometry.
2. Emergent gravity is an established research area. Induced gravity, thermodynamic gravity, analogue gravity, quantum-many-body spacetime, condensate cosmology, matrix models, and scalar-tensor threshold effects already explore neighbouring ideas.

The proposed contribution is narrower:

> A local physical system may admit more than one stable relational configuration. When declared control parameters cross a critical surface, the selected configuration may change discontinuously. Matter trajectories, light propagation, clock relations, and inertial response would then change together because they are outputs of one effective geometry, not because several independent forces were adjusted separately.

This becomes physical science only if it yields quantitative residuals beyond the strongest conventional electromagnetic, thermal, plasma, optical, acoustic, mechanical, scalar-tensor, and general-relativistic explanations.

## 2. Layer before law

MKUFT uses typed layer addresses:

```text
S -> I -> P -> O -> S
```

- `S` = substrate or source-potential;
- `I` = information, relation, constraint, address, and admissible transition;
- `P` = physical expression;
- `O` = observer-positioned registration and record.

These are modelling addresses, not extra spatial dimensions.

The layer-before-law proposal is that a law observed at `P` may be the effective regularity of a deeper relational condition rather than the final primitive rule. The ordinary-physics limit remains compulsory.

In this module, **relational geometry** means the complete constraint structure determining which physical paths, couplings, and transitions are admissible. It is not assumed to be a Lorentzian metric at the deepest level. An effective spacetime metric is one possible `P`-layer output.

```text
relational constraints
-> selected stable geometry
-> effective matter, light, and clock behaviour
```

## 3. LUCY threshold chain

```text
LUCY threshold -> LUCY-1 membrane -> LUCY-2 sustained pocket
```

- **LUCY**: the critical condition at which a candidate relational state becomes dynamically self-supporting.
- **LUCY-1**: a stable interface or membrane separating that state from its environment.
- **LUCY-2**: a sustained finite region whose effective behaviour differs from the exterior baseline.

Coherence is used operationally: mutually consistent constraints maintain a state against perturbation. **Relational closure** is the stronger term: the active constraints form a sufficiently complete loop for the state to persist and correct small deviations.

A valid implementation must define:

- state variables and spaces;
- control parameters;
- dynamical or variational rule;
- order parameter;
- critical surface;
- boundary conditions;
- ordinary limit;
- energy and entropy accounting;
- observables, scale, and sign;
- failure conditions.

Without these, LUCY remains architecture, not physics.

## 5. Minimal toy scaffold

Let `psi` be a candidate order parameter, `g` an effective metric, and `lambda` declared control parameters. A conventional scaffold is:

```text
S_eff[g,psi;lambda] = integral d^4x sqrt(-g) [
  (M0^2/2) R
  - (1/2) K(lambda) nabla_mu psi nabla^mu psi
  - V(psi;lambda)
  - (1/2) xi psi^2 R
  + L_matter(g,Phi)
]
```

This is not claimed as an original or final MKUFT action. Related forms are standard in scalar-tensor theory, cosmology, and phase-transition modelling.

Take:

```text
V(psi;lambda)
= (1/2) a(lambda) psi^2
+ (1/4) b psi^4
+ (1/6) c psi^6,
```

with `c > 0`.

Define the LUCY critical surface by equality of relevant effective minima:

```text
Delta F(lambda)
= F[psi_1,g_1;lambda] - F[psi_0,g_0;lambda]
= 0.
```

Below the surface, the baseline state is preferred. Across it, a distinct local state may become preferred. LUCY-1 corresponds to a stable interface solution. LUCY-2 requires a finite domain whose bulk gain exceeds interface and dissipation costs.

For a schematic spherical pocket:

```text
Delta E(R)
= 4 pi R^2 sigma
- (4 pi/3) R^3 Delta epsilon
+ E_coupling(R).
```

In the simplest uncoupled case, `R_c = 2 sigma / Delta epsilon`. Any geometry-coupling term must be dimensionally defined and may not be inserted merely to force stability.

The scaffold supplies threshold, boundary, pocket, hysteresis, and critical-radius concepts. It does not establish modified gravitation.

## 6. Effect regimes without two gravities

The module distinguishes regimes by observable effect, not by naming two kinds of gravity.

### Localised high-gradient regime

A configuration confined to nuclear, mesoscopic, material, or resonator scales. Shell structure, pairing, deformation, collective modes, and boundary conditions may dominate stability.

### Extended effective-geometry regime

A finite region exhibits a common change in the trajectories or phase evolution of neutral matter, light, and clocks.

The hypothesis is not:

```text
microscopic gravitational wave -> amplified macroscopic gravity
```

It is:

```text
one state-selection problem
-> different effective expressions at different scales
```

A scale bridge requires an explicit coarse-graining map:

```text
C_(ell->L): X_ell -> X_L
```

It must preserve energy, causality, and tested equivalence-principle limits. Until derived, the two regimes are only a candidate relation.

## 7. Neutron-rich superheavy nuclei as a candidate seed class

Superheavy nuclear lifetime is highly configuration-sensitive. Liquid-drop trends favour fission, while shell corrections, pairing, and deformation can raise fission barriers and lengthen lifetimes. The location and lifetime of the island of enhanced stability remain model-dependent.

Terrestrial fusion currently reaches neutron-deficient superheavy isotopes. Reviews note that available stable beams and actinide targets cannot directly reach the most neutron-rich region. Astrophysical r-process environments can traverse nuclei far from laboratory access; modern calculations may reach fission termination around mass numbers of roughly `275-300`. Survival to long-lived trans-actinide species remains uncertain and depends on poorly known masses, separation energies, beta-decay rates, and fission barriers.

Scientifically permissible:

> Extreme astrophysical environments may create neutron-rich superheavy configurations unavailable to current terrestrial synthesis, and shell effects may produce lifetimes very different from measured neutron-deficient isotopes.

Not scientifically established:

> Such a nucleus therefore controls gravitation.

In LUCY, a nuclear state is at most a candidate seed or control variable. The cross-layer coupling is unknown and may be zero.

A nucleosynthesis exposure may be written:

```text
Theta_r = tau_env n_n <sigma v>.
```

This describes neutron-capture exposure. It is not a LUCY or gravitational coupling.

## 8. Specific LUCY hypothesis

Let `q(x)` be a dimensionless relational-closure order parameter, with `q = 0` the baseline state and `q != 0` a candidate closed state. Let `lambda` collect material, geometric, electromagnetic, thermodynamic, and environmental controls.

### Base hypothesis

There exists a critical surface `Sigma_L` in control-parameter space such that:

```text
q = 0 -> q = q_*
```

produces a stable boundary and a correlated change in more than one independent class of physical observable.

### Strong gravitational hypothesis

After known material and field effects are removed, the `q != 0` state modifies an effective geometry experienced by neutral matter and electromagnetic signals in a composition-independent manner consistent with a declared `g_eff(q)`.

Optical distortion alone is insufficient. Plasma, thermal gradients, nonlinear optics, electromagnetic forces, acoustic fields, vibration, and sensor artefacts can all create boundary-like phenomena. A metric interpretation rises only when one model explains independent matter, light, and clock observables with the same boundary, sign, timing, and threshold.

## 9. Discriminating predictions

### 9.1 Threshold and hysteresis

The response should be nonlinear in a pre-registered control parameter and may show hysteresis. A smooth response proportional to applied power favours ordinary leakage, heating, or known field response.

### 9.2 Common boundary

Optical phase, neutral test-mass motion, clock comparison, and fluid transport should change at one mapped boundary. Separate boundaries or unrelated onset times weaken the unified-geometry claim.

### 9.3 Geometry dependence

After controlling electromagnetic cavity modes, thermal transport, stress, and mass distribution, the threshold should depend on normalised boundary geometry and obey a declared scaling law.

### 9.4 Composition and charge

A metric-like effect should couple approximately universally to neutral test bodies. Strong dependence on charge, conductivity, magnetic susceptibility, dielectric constant, or surface finish favours ordinary material effects.

### 9.5 Wavelength dependence

A metric-like optical-path change should be nearly achromatic in vacuum after dispersion is removed. Strong frequency dependence favours plasma, refractive-index, cavity, or metamaterial mechanisms.

### 9.6 Clock and phase comparison

At least two independent clock or interferometric methods should show a consistent proper-time or phase residual. No altered-geometry claim should be promoted without a timing discriminator.

### 9.7 Conservation accounting

Any apparent change in inertia, trajectory, or propagation requires complete source-energy, momentum, radiation, heat, and mechanical-reaction accounting. Boundary language is not permission to discard conservation laws.

### 9.8 Astrophysical signature

If a specific neutron-rich superheavy configuration is required, nuclear-network calculations should predict distinctive fission fragments, decay chains, or abundance residuals in r-process ejecta.

## 10. Promotion route

- **Tier 0 - mathematical consistency:** typed variables, units, action or update rule, stability, ordinary limit, causal structure.
- **Tier 1 - conventional analogue:** threshold and membrane behaviour in a known system without a gravity claim.
- **Tier 2 - cross-channel anomaly:** blinded reproducible common-boundary residual in at least two independent channels.
- **Tier 3 - metric discrimination:** one effective geometry outpredicts the strongest electromagnetic, thermal, plasma, acoustic, mechanical, and sensor models across neutral matter, light, and clocks.
- **Tier 4 - independent replication and scaling:** separate-laboratory replication of pre-registered scaling, hysteresis, composition, and wavelength predictions.

No ontology claim is permitted before Tier 3. No foundational promotion is permitted before Tier 4.

## 11. Falsifiers

The gravitational branch fails or is materially reduced if:

- known electromagnetic, thermal, optical, plasma, acoustic, mechanical, or sensor models close the observations;
- no dimensionally valid coupling from the order parameter to effective geometry can be constructed;
- general relativity and ordinary quantum field theory are not recovered in their tested regimes;
- claimed boundaries differ across measurement channels;
- neutral-matter and clock effects are absent while optical or charged effects remain;
- the response is smooth and linear where a critical transition was pre-registered;
- scaling depends on uncontrolled apparatus details rather than declared invariants;
- energy or momentum accounting fails;
- the nuclear seed is unnecessary, non-specific, or contradicted by data;
- parameters, geometry, or thresholds are selected after the result;
- an existing theory reproduces every predicted observable with equal or fewer assumptions.

A null result may not be moved to an unmeasured layer. The failed branch must be revised or removed.

## 12. Relationship to existing LUCY and MKUFT

This module preserves the base chain:

```text
LUCY threshold -> LUCY-1 membrane -> LUCY-2 sustained pocket
```

It adds:

- coherence versus relational closure;
- a conventional state-selection scaffold;
- localised and extended effect-regime separation;
- the explicit nuclear-seed boundary;
- cross-channel signatures;
- promotion gates and falsifiers.

It sharpens Layer Before Law without replacing general relativity. The question is whether the effective metric may itself be a selected `P`-layer state of a deeper typed relational architecture.

Compact rule:

> Do not amplify “gravity.” Define the state, cross the threshold, map the boundary, recover the metric, and let the observables decide whether geometry changed.
