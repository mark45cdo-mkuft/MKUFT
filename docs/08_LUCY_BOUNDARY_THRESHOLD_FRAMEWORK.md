## Abstract

LUCY — Local Unified Coherence Yield — is MKUFT's threshold architecture for the transition from an unstable local gradient to a stable boundary and, where that boundary remains dynamically supported, to a sustained region whose behaviour differs from its exterior baseline.

The canonical chain is:

```text
LUCY threshold → LUCY-1 membrane → LUCY-2 sustained pocket
```

This module distinguishes **coherence** from the stronger condition of **relational closure**. Coherence means that active components or constraints are mutually consistent enough to reinforce a candidate state. Relational closure means that the relevant constraint loop is sufficiently complete for the state to persist, resist perturbation, and regulate crossings at its boundary.

LUCY is not a claim that gravitation, inertia, light propagation, nuclear stability, cognition, or information routing are identical. It is a typed threshold architecture that can be addressed separately at informational, physical, and observer layers. Evidence cannot be inherited between those addresses without a defined carrier.

For physical applications, LUCY does not propose that a gravitational wave, particle, or force is amplified. Its stronger open hypothesis is that a local relational state may cross a threshold at which a different effective physical geometry becomes stable. Gravity-like observations would then be outputs of the selected geometry rather than the primitive object being generated.

This proposal overlaps established work on phase transitions, domain walls, emergent spacetime, induced gravity, analogue gravity, scalarisation, and collective states. Those topics are not claimed as original. The proposed MKUFT contribution is the typed LUCY conjunction: threshold, membrane, sustained pocket, relational closure, cross-channel boundary discrimination, strongest-fair-null testing, ordinary-physics recovery, and explicit falsification requirements.

## 1. Name and exact object

LUCY = **Local Unified Coherence Yield**.

The exact object is not "coherence causes anything desirable." It is:

> A declared local state crosses a pre-specified threshold, forms a stable boundary, and may sustain a finite region with reproducibly different behaviour.

Every use of LUCY must identify:

- the addressed layer;
- the state variables;
- the coherence or closure measure;
- the control parameters;
- the threshold;
- the boundary condition;
- the predicted post-threshold behaviour;
- the ordinary baseline;
- and the falsifier.

Without those items, LUCY is only descriptive language.

## 2. The canonical three-stage chain

```text
LUCY → LUCY-1 → LUCY-2
```

### 2.1 LUCY — threshold

LUCY is the critical surface at which a candidate local state becomes dynamically admissible and stable enough to support boundary formation.

Below threshold, local alignment or fluctuation may occur but no stable boundary is maintained. At threshold, the candidate state becomes self-reinforcing under declared conditions and may become a different stable or metastable solution.

### 2.2 LUCY-1 — membrane

LUCY-1 is the first post-threshold state.

A **LUCY membrane** is a stable interface separating two differently resolved local conditions. "Membrane" is functional language: it identifies a boundary that regulates crossing, coupling, routing, or state continuity. It does not require a biological membrane or a literal sheet of matter.

A valid membrane claim should specify:

- boundary thickness or resolution limit;
- lifetime;
- permeability or coupling rule;
- surface energy or maintenance cost where physical;
- response to perturbation;
- and the observables that locate the boundary.

### 2.3 LUCY-2 — sustained pocket

LUCY-2 is a finite region maintained by the LUCY-1 boundary.

The preferred canonical term is **sustained pocket** or **sustained local region**. "LUCY field" may be used only where a field is mathematically defined in the addressed domain.

A sustained pocket requires more than a momentary boundary. It must maintain a distinguishable internal state for a declared time and under declared noise, loading, and environmental conditions.

## 3. Coherence and relational closure

Coherence and closure are related but not interchangeable.

### 3.1 Coherence

Coherence is alignment or mutual consistency among the active constraints, phases, routes, or state variables relevant to the addressed system.

Operational indicators may include reduced phase dispersion, reduced routing conflict, increased correlation, stable ordering, reduced transition noise, or persistent low-cost traversal.

### 3.2 Relational closure

Relational closure is achieved when the active relations form a sufficiently complete loop to maintain the state and regulate its boundary.

A coherent pattern may still be transient. A closed pattern has a self-maintaining route:

```text
state → boundary response → correction / reinforcement → retained state
```

Closure adds persistence, reciprocal constraint, perturbation correction, boundary maintenance, and a complete enough action-response loop.

The stronger physical LUCY claim begins at closure, not at resemblance, synchrony, or high correlation alone.

### 3.3 Deeper constraint-closure hypothesis

Coherence may not be the primitive transition variable. It may be an observable consequence of a deeper condition in which a network of constraints becomes mutually satisfiable, reciprocally supporting, and sufficiently closed to persist under perturbation.

The candidate cross-domain transition grammar is:

```text
constraint compatibility
→ reciprocal support
→ closure threshold
→ coherent state
→ stable boundary
→ sustained local regime
→ domain-specific emergent behaviour
```

This does not assert that nuclear stability, collective physical phases, biological organisation, cognition, and effective geometry share one mechanism or one equation. It proposes that they may instantiate a common structural transition: a previously unstable set of relations becomes self-supporting enough to maintain identity, boundary, correction, and continued operation.

In this reading:

- coherence is the measurable alignment of the realised state;
- closure is the condition that lets the state persist;
- the boundary is the physical or operational expression of that closure;
- and the post-threshold behaviour remains domain-specific.

This hypothesis is retained only if it adds predictive discrimination beyond established domain models. It fails as a physical addition when "constraint closure" merely redescribes stability after the fact, cannot be operationalised independently, or transfers mechanisms between domains without a defined carrier.

LUCY retains **coherence** in its name and operational measures. Constraint closure is the deeper candidate interpretation, not a silent renaming or completed derivation.

### 3.4 O-layer closure address

Within the recursive MKUFT architecture, `O` is the address at which a realised configuration becomes registered as a sufficiently determinate relation to complete the loop and become available for recurrence into `S`.

The source-level cycle is:

```text
S potential
→ I constraint / address
→ P realisation
→ O registration / closure
→ S updated possibility
```

Applied locally to LUCY:

```text
constraint compatibility at I
→ coherent realisation at P
→ registration and closure at O
→ boundary maintenance at P
→ sustained regime
```

This placement does not restrict `O` to a human witness or conscious biological observer. `O` is the observer-positioned function of registration, resolution, feedback, or durable state-recording that closes the relevant action-response relation. Depending on the system, that function may be instantiated by a measurement arrangement, an internal feedback path, a boundary response, an environmental coupling, or a self-model capable of returning the realised state into the next cycle.

The causal work may be distributed across `I`, `P`, and `O`, but MKUFT assigns the completed closure address to `O`: coherence prepares and expresses the candidate state; `O` completes its registration as a closed relation; the maintained boundary preserves the resulting regime.

This is an architectural hypothesis, not a declaration that human observation creates physical reality or that every measurement causes a LUCY transition. An `O`-layer event counts as closure only when it completes a previously open relation and its feedback or registration is required for the state to persist, recurse, or remain operationally determinate.

The placement must add discrimination. A developed model should identify the relevant `O` channel and predict what changes when that registration or feedback is removed, delayed, corrupted, or decoupled. If no identifiable `O` function is required and the assignment produces no different prediction from a purely post-hoc description of stability, the proposed closure address requires revision.

## 4. Layer discipline

MKUFT uses the recursive architecture:

```text
S → I → P → O → S
```

where:

- `S` = substrate or source-potential;
- `I` = information, relation, address, constraint, and admissible transition;
- `P` = physical expression and measurable interaction;
- `O` = observer-positioned registration, state resolution, feedback or record, and the closure address through which a realised `P` state becomes available for recurrence into `S`.

These are typed addresses, not four ordinary spatial dimensions.

LUCY can be addressed at multiple layers, but the variables and evidence must change honestly:

- `LUCY(I)` — informational, routing, or relational constraint formation;
- `LUCY(P)` — material or physical realisation, boundary transition, and maintenance;
- `LUCY(O)` — registration or observer-positioned closure of the realised relation, where operationally defined.

A result at one address does not prove a result at another.

> No untyped inheritance.

An informational threshold cannot be presented as evidence of a new physical field. An observer-state effect cannot be promoted to a material metric change without an independently measured P-layer carrier.

## 6. Minimal threshold notation

A domain-specific LUCY model may define a yield index `Y_L`:

```text
Y_L(x,t) = χ_L ||∇τ_L(x,t)||² / [N_L(x,t) + ε_L]
```

with a pre-specified threshold:

```text
Y_L(x,t) ≥ Y_*
```

where, for the addressed domain:

- `τ_L` is a declared scalar gradient variable;
- `χ_L` is a declared coupling or scale factor;
- `N_L` is a declared noise, disorder, or competing-load measure;
- `ε_L > 0` regularises the denominator;
- `Y_*` is the pre-registered threshold.

This equation is a template, not a universal law. It is valid only when every term is defined, the units or normalisation are consistent, the measure is not selected after observing the result, and threshold crossing predicts a distinct post-threshold state.

Where curl-like behaviour is required, a vector potential must be introduced. A scalar `τ_L` cannot be assigned a curl by notation alone.

## 7. State-selection scaffold

A conventional toy scaffold can represent the transition without pretending the scaffold is uniquely MKUFT.

Let `q(x,t)` be an order parameter and `λ` a set of declared control parameters. Define an effective functional:

```text
F[q; λ] = ∫_V [ (κ_q/2)|∇q|² + V(q;λ) ] dV + F_boundary + F_environment
```

A simple potential may be:

```text
V(q;λ) = (a(λ)/2)q² + (b/4)q⁴ + (c/6)q⁶
```

with `c > 0`.

The realised state is:

```text
q_* = argmin_q F[q;λ]
```

The LUCY critical surface `Σ_L` occurs where the preferred solution changes or where a metastable state becomes dynamically maintainable:

```text
ΔF(λ) = F[q_1;λ] - F[q_0;λ] = 0
```

This captures threshold, competing stable states, hysteresis, interface formation, and closure. It does not prove a new physical coupling.

## 8. Membrane and pocket energetics

For a roughly spherical physical pocket of radius `R`, a schematic nucleation cost is:

```text
ΔE(R) = 4πR²σ - (4π/3)R³Δε + E_load(R) + E_diss(R)
```

where:

- `σ` = interface or membrane energy per unit area;
- `Δε` = bulk energy-density difference between states;
- `E_load` = declared environmental or coupling cost;
- `E_diss` = loss or maintenance cost.

In the simplest uncoupled case:

```text
R_c = 2σ / Δε
```

is the critical radius above which volume gain can exceed surface cost.

This is standard nucleation grammar. LUCY uses it as a discipline: a sustained pocket must pay its boundary and maintenance costs. "Coherence" is not free energy.

## 9. Gravity-like behaviour as output, not primitive

General relativity already models gravitation through spacetime geometry rather than an ordinary Newtonian force. LUCY does not claim that correction as new.

The deeper open proposal is:

> A local relational configuration may select or sustain an effective geometry. Matter paths, light paths, clock relations, and inertial behaviour would then be correlated outputs of that geometry.

The proposed chain is:

```text
constraint configuration
→ relational closure
→ selected effective geometry
→ matter / light / clock observables
```

It is not:

```text
small gravitational wave
→ amplifier
→ large gravitational force
```

No claim is made here that the underlying object is a wave, particle, or force. "Geometry" itself may also be an effective P-layer description rather than the final substrate ontology.

### 9.1 Two effect regimes without two gravities

The framework may distinguish regimes by effect and scale:

- **local high-gradient regime:** a confined material, nuclear, mesoscopic, or resonator state whose stability is strongly configuration-dependent;
- **extended effective-geometry regime:** a finite region in which neutral matter, light, and clocks share one altered boundary model.

These are not named as separate gravities. A connection between them requires an explicit scale map:

```text
C_{ℓ→L}: X_ℓ → X_L
```

The map must show how microscopic configuration becomes a macroscopic order parameter while preserving causality, energy accounting, and tested equivalence-principle limits. Until then, the relation is a hypothesis and the coupling may be zero.

## 10. Extreme configuration-sensitive matter

Some physical systems are relevant because their stability depends sharply on configuration rather than bulk quantity alone.

Neutron-rich superheavy nuclei are one candidate class. Their survival may depend on shell corrections, pairing, deformation, and fission barriers. Terrestrial fusion currently produces neutron-deficient isotopes, while astrophysical rapid-neutron-capture environments may reach nuclear configurations unavailable in current laboratories.

The permitted statement is:

> Extreme astrophysical environments may produce configuration-sensitive nuclei not reached by present terrestrial synthesis, and their lifetimes may differ sharply from measured neutron-deficient isotopes.

The prohibited inheritance is:

> Therefore such a nucleus alters gravitation.

No accepted nuclear model supplies that coupling. Within LUCY, extreme nuclei are only a candidate seed class. The physical coupling must be derived and may be absent.

A nucleosynthesis exposure parameter may be written:

```text
Θ_r = τ_env n_n ⟨σv⟩
```

This describes neutron-capture exposure. It is not a geometry-changing parameter and must not be made one by analogy.

## 11. Geometry, scale, and threshold

Scaling may change category rather than merely increase magnitude. Known examples include laser threshold, superconducting transition, critical nucleation, collective modes, and spontaneous ordering.

LUCY therefore permits the hypothesis that a smoothly varied control parameter may produce a discontinuous state change. But scale alone is not sufficient. A valid scaling claim requires the relevant extensive and intensive variables, coherence length, finite-size corrections, geometry and boundary dependence, dissipation, critical exponent or declared non-universal law, and ordinary limit.

A proposed collective control parameter may depend on population, coherence, and geometry, but its terms must carry correct dimensions and independent meaning. An `N²` term cannot be inserted merely because coherent amplitudes sometimes add quadratically.

## 12. Discriminating physical signature

A conventional force or material effect and a selected effective geometry make different predictions.

A serious P-layer LUCY claim requires several normally distinct observables to change at the same spatial boundary and control threshold.

Candidate channels include:

- neutral test-mass motion;
- optical phase and path;
- clock comparison;
- fluid convection;
- electromagnetic propagation;
- thermal transport;
- material stress;
- pressure;
- and radiation or plasma diagnostics.

The load-bearing signature is:

> One boundary, one onset, one control threshold, and one model predicting the correlated residuals.

A change in only one channel normally favours a domain-specific explanation.

## 13. Required discriminators

### 13.1 Threshold and hysteresis

Pre-register the control parameter and predicted onset. A sharp transition or hysteresis may support a phase-change model. A smooth power-proportional response favours heating, field leakage, or ordinary nonlinear response unless otherwise derived.

### 13.2 Common boundary

Independent sensors must locate the same boundary within declared uncertainty. Optical, mechanical, and timing effects that occupy different regions do not support one geometry.

### 13.3 Geometry dependence

After controlling for electromagnetic cavity modes, stress, thermal transport, mass distribution, and fluid flow, normalised boundary geometry should predict threshold or effect class. Geometrically similar systems should follow a declared scaling relation.

### 13.4 Composition and charge

A metric-like residual should couple approximately universally to neutral bodies. Strong dependence on charge, conductivity, magnetic susceptibility, dielectric constant, or surface finish favours conventional interactions.

### 13.5 Wavelength

After plasma and material dispersion are removed, a metric-like optical-path residual should be approximately achromatic. Strong wavelength dependence favours refraction, absorption, scattering, plasma, or resonant material effects.

### 13.6 Clocks and phase

At least two independent timing or phase methods should show a consistent residual before altered effective geometry is claimed.

### 13.7 Conservation

Source energy, heat, radiation, momentum transfer, mechanical reaction, and entropy production must be measured. A boundary transition is not an exemption from accounting.

### 13.8 Astrophysical residue

If a particular extreme nuclear configuration is required, network calculations should predict distinctive abundance, decay-chain, or fission-fragment signatures. Failure to find the predicted signature as nuclear data improve weakens that seed branch.

## 14. Strongest fair null

LUCY cannot be supported by comparing it only with a weak baseline.

For each experiment, construct the strongest reasonable conventional explanation set:

- electromagnetic;
- electrohydrodynamic;
- plasma;
- thermal;
- acoustic;
- mechanical;
- fluid-dynamic;
- optical;
- material;
- sensor and reconstruction artefact;
- general-relativistic or scalar-tensor where relevant;
- and combined multi-factor nulls.

The proposed LUCY model carries weight only if it predicts held-out observations better than the strongest fair null under matched information, compute, calibration, and parameter freedom.

## 15. Evidence and promotion gates

### Tier 0 — mathematical consistency

Define spaces, variables, dimensions, update rule, stability, ordinary limit, and causality.

### Tier 1 — known analogue reproduction

Demonstrate threshold, membrane, and pocket behaviour in an established system without claiming new gravitation.

### Tier 2 — reproducible cross-channel residual

Under blinded and sham-controlled conditions, observe one mapped boundary in at least two independent channels.

### Tier 3 — effective-geometry discrimination

Show that one effective geometry predicts neutral matter, light, and clock behaviour better than the strongest conventional alternatives.

### Tier 4 — independent replication and scaling

A separate laboratory reproduces the effect and the pre-registered geometry, threshold, composition, wavelength, and hysteresis predictions.

No foundational or ontology claim is promoted before these gates are met.

## 16. Ordinary-physics recovery

A developed LUCY model must recover ordinary behaviour when:

- `Y_L < Y_*`;
- the order parameter remains in the baseline minimum;
- the membrane is unstable;
- the pocket radius remains below critical size;
- the proposed cross-layer coupling is zero;
- or the measured residual is below declared sensitivity.

In those conditions, the addressed domain is described by its established ordinary theory.

LUCY must also recover general relativity and quantum field theory within their tested domains before it can claim a deeper physical account.

## 17. Falsification and reduction

A LUCY branch is weakened, reduced, or rejected when:

- the threshold cannot be operationally defined;
- the post-threshold state is not reproducibly distinct;
- no stable boundary is located;
- different channels identify different boundaries;
- effects scale with charge, temperature, pressure, vibration, or apparatus variables already sufficient to explain them;
- the proposed geometry does not outperform the strongest conventional model;
- a dimensionally valid coupling to physical observables cannot be constructed;
- energy or momentum accounting fails;
- claimed scaling is fitted only after the result is known;
- the same notation changes meaning across layers;
- a nuclear or material seed is non-specific or unnecessary;
- held-out predictions fail;
- or the framework adds no discriminatory power beyond existing formalisms.

A failed physical branch is not rescued by relocating the effect to an unmeasured informational or observer layer.

## 18. What LUCY is not

LUCY is not:

- a new force by declaration;
- a gravity particle;
- a gravity wave;
- a universal field substance;
- a synonym for consciousness;
- proof of extra dimensions;
- a licence to treat analogy as mechanism;
- free energy;
- a replacement for general relativity;
- a replacement for quantum field theory;
- or a label for every coherent system.

LUCY is a threshold architecture. Each physical application must earn its own mechanism and evidence.
