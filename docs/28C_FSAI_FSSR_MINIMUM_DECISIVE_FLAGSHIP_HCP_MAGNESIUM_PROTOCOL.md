## 1. Why this protocol exists

MKUFT now has enough experiment-governance machinery that adding another general methodological layer is less useful than forcing one central relation into a native test that can return a clean positive, null, contraction, or failure.

The relation selected is the **Future-Sufficient Address Invariant / Future-Splitting State Recruitment** family:

> A state representation is sufficient for a declared future only while histories it calls the same do not split into materially different admissible futures beyond the declared tolerance. If they do split, the model must recruit the smallest physically typed distinction that restores held-out closure; if they do not, the simpler state is provisionally preserved.

Module 33S7 already names history-dependent constitutive mechanics in HCP magnesium, or a comparable material with rich microstate-resolved continuation data, as a prospective flagship. This document turns that statement into a minimum decisive experiment pack.

The point is **not** to rediscover that magnesium twins, detwins, hardens, localises strain, or remembers loading history. Those are established materials-science facts. The test is whether the FSAI/FSSR procedure adds prospective experimental value beyond strong native constitutive modelling and ordinary experimental design.

## 2. Exact claim under test

The primary methodological claim is:

> **A strong reduced constitutive state that is adequate in a baseline regime can become prospectively inadequate under a lawful separating reversal; an FSSR-selected challenge can expose that loss of state sufficiency, and a smallest predeclared measurable physical augmentation can restore held-out predictive closure.**

A stronger challenge-design claim is tested separately:

> **A challenge selected to expose false state equivalence can produce more useful state-adequacy discrimination, under matched experimental budget, than a challenge selected only to identify parameters of the current model.**

A stronger mechanism-localisation claim is tested separately again:

> **The state augmentation that restores closure can be tied to a physically measurable twinning/slip/history descriptor and survive remove/restore or matched deformation tests rather than remaining an arbitrary latent coordinate.**

A prospective-lead claim is also separate:

> **Where the microstructural mechanism becomes future-discriminating before the conventional macroscopic constitutive marker is overt, state recruitment can occur first: `lambda_R < lambda_T`.**

None of these claims inherits success from another automatically.

## 3. What success would not establish

Even a full positive result would not establish:

- that MKUFT discovered twinning or detwinning;
- that HCP magnesium requires a new physical law;
- that information is an autonomous physical force;
- that the S–I–P–O address is a new spatial dimension;
- that the FSAI notation supersedes crystal plasticity, constitutive modelling, active learning, optimal experimental design, observability, hysteresis, or state-estimation theory;
- that one materials result validates MKUFT in biology, AI, quantum foundations, gravity, consciousness, or metaphysics;
- or that MKUFT is a completed unified field theory.

A positive result can support only the experimentally earned methodological/mechanism statement at the declared material, loading regime, target, horizon, and measurement resolution.

## 4. Native system and prior-art boundary

Use a textured HCP magnesium alloy with a mature cyclic/reversal literature, with **AZ31/AZ31B as the default reference system** unless the executing laboratory has a better-characterised equivalent.

The choice is practical rather than proprietary. Existing work already supplies the native phenomena and instrumentation needed to make the FSSR test non-trivial:

- twinning–detwinning constitutive models for HCP polycrystals: DOI `10.1016/j.msea.2012.06.038`;

These are **native ownership anchors and feasibility evidence**, not evidence for MKUFT. The experiment must beat or add something to the strongest fair domain-native account rather than renaming established hysteresis or internal variables.

## 6. Primary future target

Use **one primary future object** for the principal claim:

```math
q = sigma_rev(epsilon)
```

—the reverse-loading macroscopic stress trajectory over a preregistered strain window after the challenged state.

Sample the trajectory on a fixed grid:

```math
y = [sigma(epsilon_1),...,sigma(epsilon_m)].
```

A default trajectory discrepancy is the covariance-standardised distance

```math
d_q(y,y')
=
\sqrt{\frac{1}{m}(y-y')^T Sigma_0^{-1}(y-y')},
```

where `Sigma_0` is estimated only from the calibration/development data and frozen before confirmation. A laboratory may predeclare another defensible trajectory distance, but it may not choose the distance after seeing which one makes the split largest.

Set `epsilon_q` from baseline replicate variability plus a prospectively declared smallest material difference of interest. Do not define the tolerance by the confirmatory cross-history split.

Secondary readouts may include:

- twin volume fraction;
- twin-variant distribution;
- texture evolution;
- local strain fields from DIC/HRDIC;
- diffraction peak/intensity or lattice-strain observables;
- slip/twin activity summaries from a separately validated crystal-plasticity model;
- hardening/back-stress proxies;
- spatial localisation or twin-band descriptors.

Secondary readouts localise the mechanism and test the repair. They do not replace the frozen primary target after the result is known.

## 7. State hierarchy — no straw-man baseline

### 7.1 Calibration state `Theta_macro`

A deliberately coarse macroscopic state may be used only as a calibration surface, for example current stress, strain, temperature and loading direction.

A history split under `Theta_macro` is expected in many cyclic magnesium regimes and does **not** constitute the flagship result.

### 7.2 Confirmatory state `Theta_0`

The confirmatory object is the **strongest practical reduced domain-native constitutive state selected before confirmatory data**.

`Theta_0` must include every state variable that the chosen serious ordinary model normally requires and that is available under the agreed measurement budget. If a modern twinning–detwinning model already owns residual twin fraction, hardening state, texture or another internal coordinate, that variable cannot be artificially omitted merely to manufacture FSSR success.

### 7.3 Flexible ordinary baselines

Also include, within matched information and training budgets where feasible:

- a stronger history-aware constitutive baseline;
- a flexible nonparametric or machine-learned history model;
- a high-capacity latent-state model whose held-out performance is evaluated without interpreting arbitrary latent coordinates as mechanisms.

If one of these closes the declared future more simply and with equal or better held-out performance, the MKUFT-specific claim must contract.

### 7.4 Independent continuation

The model whose state is being tested cannot certify itself.

Confirmatory continuation must come from at least one independent source:

```text
physical experiment
OR separately validated higher-fidelity microstructure-resolved simulator/model
```

For the strongest claim, physical experiment is the decisive continuation and the higher-fidelity model is a localisation/deformation aid rather than the sole judge.

## 9. Challenge family and the question-behind-the-question test

FSSR does not simply ask for a strong reversal. It asks which lawful future best tests whether `Theta_0` is really a state for `q`.

Create one bounded admissible challenge family `U`, preferably by varying only a small number of native controls such as:

- reversal direction;
- reversal amplitude;
- bounded dwell before reversal;
- one preregistered loading-rate level if rate is scientifically required.

Keep temperature, fixture, specimen geometry and unrelated conditions fixed unless they are themselves the declared experimental coordinate.

### 9.1 FSSR challenge

On development data only, select

```math
u_F
in
argmax_{u in U}
J_split(u;Theta_0),
```

where `J_split` rewards future separation **inside matched `Theta_0` fibres** while penalising measurement instability and challenge-induced mismatch.

Freeze `u_F` before confirmatory continuation.

### 9.2 Parameter-information comparator

Using the same development data, challenge family, experimental budget and access to the current ordinary model, select a comparator

```math
u_PI
```

that maximises a standard parameter-information or parameter-identification objective.

The exact native method may be Fisher-information, posterior-entropy reduction, active-learning uncertainty reduction, or another predeclared method appropriate to the executing model.

### 9.3 Standard-practice comparator

Where useful, add one ordinary laboratory reversal `u_STD` selected by domain convention before confirmatory outcomes are inspected.

### 9.4 Fair comparison

`u_F`, `u_PI` and `u_STD` must receive matched:


If `u_F` and `u_PI` are equivalent under fair tuning and produce equivalent held-out discrimination, the **separate FSSR challenge-selection claim is NULL**, even if the underlying state-recruitment event is real.

## 13. Empirical FSSR residual and closure test

For matched histories under a frozen challenge, estimate the future-splitting residual using the preregistered trajectory discrepancy:

```math
\widehat R_q(Theta_0;u)
=
\operatorname{UpperTail}_{pre}
\{d_q(y_h,y_h') : D_Theta(Theta_0(h),Theta_0(h')) <= tau_Theta\}.
```

`UpperTail_pre` must be fixed before confirmation — for example a specified high quantile with uncertainty or another robust worst-case estimator justified by the sample design.

State insufficiency for the declared experiment requires

```math
\widehat R_q(Theta_0;u_F) > epsilon_q
```

with the uncertainty/precision rule preregistered.

A recruitment candidate `c` closes only when the held-out repaired state satisfies

```math
\widehat R_q(Theta_0 \oplus c;u_F) <= epsilon_q.
```

The **ablation/reopening check** is

```text
Theta_0 + c* closes
→ remove c* while keeping the frozen repaired model otherwise unchanged
→ future failure reappears beyond tolerance
→ restore c*
→ closure returns on an independent or held-out continuation
```

This is a predictive-state deformation test. Physical mechanism ownership requires the additional Stage-3 native deformation burden.

## 15. Probe-induction control

The separating challenge may itself create twinning or another microstructural distinction rather than reveal a distinction already carrying different futures.

Therefore Stage 1 must define at least one probe-induction control, such as:

- a lower-amplitude nested challenge;
- a passive/pre-challenge microstructure measurement where feasible;
- a challenge series that identifies whether divergence existed in predictive state before the overt transition;
- a high-fidelity continuation showing the candidate coordinate was already different before the challenge;
- or another domain-native method capable of separating revelation from induction.

If the split vanishes when probe induction is controlled, the hidden-state claim contracts to a challenge-created transition.

## 16. Conventional macroscopic marker and recruitment lead

Define the conventional marker before Stage 2 from a macroscopic constitutive criterion, not from the candidate repair itself.

A default operational form is:

```text
lambda_T = earliest loading coordinate at which the macroscopic trajectory
           departs from the preregistered baseline/slip-dominant constitutive
           envelope by more than epsilon_T for the required consecutive increments.
```

The exact envelope, `epsilon_T`, and persistence rule are Stage-1 objects and must be frozen.

Define

```math
L = lambda_T - lambda_R,
```

where `lambda_R` is the earliest prospectively confirmed loading coordinate at which `Theta_0` requires `c*` to preserve the declared future within `epsilon_q`.

A positive lead claim requires `L>0` with preregistered uncertainty. If the intervals overlap or the lead disappears under replication, the prospective-lead claim is NULL even if state recruitment itself remains valid.

## 17. Strongest fair null family

The flagship must actively attempt to die under at least these alternatives.

### N1 — state mismatch

The matched histories were not actually equivalent under `Theta_0`. Tighter matching or a variable already owned by the ordinary state removes the split.

**Verdict:** ordinary matching failure; no FSSR recruitment credit.

### N2 — stronger ordinary constitutive state

A serious domain-native model already closes the future using established internal variables.

**Verdict:** native model wins; MKUFT state-recruitment delta contracts or becomes translation only.

### N3 — flexible history model

A matched-information flexible history-aware model closes held-out futures as well or better without the FSSR repair discipline.

**Verdict:** test whether FSSR still improves minimality, interpretability, probe efficiency or preservation/reopening. If not, methodological delta is NULL.

### N4 — challenge/boundary mismatch

Dose/amplitude/rate/dwell/fixture/thermal/alignment differences carry the split.

**Verdict:** readdress the challenge/boundary; no hidden-state promotion.

### N5 — measurement/registration artefact

DIC/EBSD/XRD acquisition, registration, interpolation or analysis pipeline creates the apparent divergence.

**Verdict:** no state failure until instrument null is removed.

### N6 — ordinary stochastic spread

The split lies inside the frozen variability model.

**Verdict:** preserve `Theta_0` for the declared scope.

### N7 — probe induction

`u_F` creates the candidate mechanism rather than exposing a pre-existing future distinction.

**Verdict:** state-recruitment interpretation contracts.

### N8 — parameter-design equivalence

`u_PI` selects the same/equivalent challenge and achieves equal held-out state discrimination under matched budget.

**Verdict:** FSSR challenge-design delta is NULL; any valid state-recruitment result remains separately scored.

### N9 — arbitrary latent repair

Only a high-dimensional latent coordinate closes, with no stable physical descriptor or invariant subspace surviving reparameterisation/deformation.

**Verdict:** predictive repair may stand; mechanism localisation fails.

### N10 — repair overfit

`c*` closes development data but not fresh confirmation or removal/restoration.

**Verdict:** recruitment fails.

### N11 — generic intervention damage

Any comparable disturbance produces the same trajectory degradation.

**Verdict:** relation-specific ownership fails.

### N12 — compensation

A redundant slip/twin route preserves the parent trajectory despite local deformation.

**Verdict:** local load may be real while parent closure remains intact; do not promote local break into global failure.
