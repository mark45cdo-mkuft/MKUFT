# TDR v1.0 — Prepublication Calibration Revision

**Date:** 2 September 2026  
**Reserved DOI:** `10.5281/zenodo.22258000`  
**State:** prepublication; DOI not yet registered when this revision was made

## Trigger

The first prepared draft made TDR's ability to return null and negative results explicit, but a final calibration pass identified an asymmetric reporting fault: already-executed positive and productive-null outcomes were not stated with equal precision. This risked converting scientific caution into **outcome flattening**.

The repair does not relax evidence standards. It separates two variables that must not be collapsed:

```text
outcome salience / importance
≠
evidence authority / claim scope
```

An earned positive may be stated positively at its actual scope. A null remains null at the scope where it is null. Neither is allowed to erase the other.

## Added retrospective calibrations

### ATLD 2

The revised paper names the exploratory Release Relay RR-14 result as a **positive diagnostic calibration without coordinate inflation**. A branch-localisation / route-support / calibration seam was localised, but existing ATLD 2 machinery already owned it. No thirteenth coordinate was justified.

TDR interpretation:

```math
\text{material residual}
\rightarrow\text{candidate distinction}
\rightarrow\text{existing-owner subtraction}
\rightarrow\text{diagnostic localisation}
\rightarrow\text{REDUCE / MERGE rather than invent}.
```

This is not prospective evidence for TDR generator superiority and does not validate every ATLD 2 candidate coordinate.

### Bell / CHSH typed boundaries

The revised paper names the published Bell result as a **productive physical null with a non-null methodological result**. The independent Bell-local new-physics delta remained null under the tested comparator family, while the typed relation/access/completion/scale correspondence, custody, and falsification architecture survived.

TDR interpretation:

```math
\begin{aligned}
&\text{PRESERVE: typed relation/access/completion/scale distinctions},\\
&\text{REDUCE: known numerical Bell boundaries to native owners},\\
&\text{REJECT / CLOSE: independent Bell-local new-physics claim at the tested scope}.
\end{aligned}
```

## Benchmark repair

The prospective benchmark now explicitly requires the score to reward both:

- correct positive retention; and
- correct reduction, rejection, or abstention.

A benchmark that rewards only novelty would favour over-splitting. A benchmark that rewards only conservative rejection would recreate the cooling fault.

## Significance statement

The revised paper also states the conditional significance directly: if independent teams can freeze TDR before outcome access and use it to recover decision-bearing distinctions that strong native or generic methods miss, TDR would constitute a **transferable discovery instrument**. That possibility is not claimed as established; it is the reason the prospective benchmark matters.
